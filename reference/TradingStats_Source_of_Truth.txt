HOW TO INTERPRET PROBABILITIES
==================================================
 
Base rates, not forecasts.
"70% hit rate" means the event occurred in 70% of past cases under similar conditions — not that today has a 70% chance.
 
Sample size matters.
Check n in the tooltip. n=15 is noisy. n=500+ is stable.
 
Confidence intervals widen with small n.
65% with CI [45–82%] tells you less than 65% with CI [62–68%].
 
Conditional probability ≠ base rate.
condProb updates as time and price move. Use the live value, not the static table.
 
Edge ≠ conviction.
A 62% edge with discipline beats 85% with bad sizing. Probability is input, not a trade plan.
 
Non-directional events (retest, fill) do not signal trade direction — context only.

 Edge Accuracy (rebuilt June 10, 2026 — full trust & calibration layer):
Purpose: Answers "Can I actually trust this %?" for every module.
Methodology: Walk-forward backtest over ~2 years of prior-only data — zero look-ahead, no cherry-picking.
Access: Command Center → scroll to Edge Accuracy block.
 
Filters (top right):
- Period: 1M / 3M / 6M / 2Y — applies to win rate display
- Min Signal Strength slider: 50%–90% — recomputes live, filters out low-confidence calls
 
Per-module columns:
- Win Rate + 95% CI — actual hit rate with confidence interval. Modules under n=30 are visually dimmed (noisy sample — read with extra skepticism).
- Calibration Grade (A–D) — gap between stated % and actual outcome. A = honest. D = stated % is misleading. A low grade doesn't mean the module is useless — it means apply a discount to the stated probability.
- Base — coin-flip or baseline for that module type (typically 50%)
- Edge — win rate minus base. The real number. This is what beats randomness.
- Trust Score — overall reliability score combining edge + calibration. Primary sort key. Higher = more dependable.
- Sample (n) — dataset size. Check this before weighting any module.
- Drift — sparkline showing edge curve over time. Flat/rising = stable. Declining = degrading.

- Win rate by day of week: Mon–Fri — identifies if a module prints better on specific days. Use this during pre-market prep when a module is active.
 
Confluence signal:
- When 4+ directional modules align the same way, historical resolution rate is displayed
- This is a measured consensus signal — use it as a conviction input alongside ATR and RSI, not as a standalone entry trigger
- Appears automatically when threshold is met; no manual action needed
 
How to read it in practice:
- Trust Score is the primary filter. High trust + high edge = lean on it. High win rate + low calibration grade = the % is misleading, size down.
- Check the Long/Short split before assuming a module applies to your trade direction. Some modules have strong Long edge but weak Short edge (or vice versa).
- Check the day-of-week row — if today is a historically weak day for a module, weight it less even if the probability looks good.
- n matters. A 90% win rate on n=16 (e.g., Weekly Open) is far less stable than 87% on n=85. Treat low-n modules as directional context only, not high-confidence edges.
- Confluence signal at 4+ modules is meaningful. Note it in Part B session read when it fires.
 
Calibration grades — what they mean operationally:
  A: Stated % closely matches actual outcome. Use the number as shown.
  B: Minor gap. Small discount appropriate.
  C: Moderate gap. Read the % directionally, not literally.
  D: Significant gap. Module may still be useful directionally but stated probability is unreliable — do not use the % for sizing decisions.

CHART OVERLAYS — DETAIL
==================================================
 
PRISMA ZONES
Quantile reaction zones from historical bar ranges.
Six timeframes: 1H, 4H, 8H, 12H, D, W
Two modes: Extension (EXT) when price breaks prior bar range, Interior (INT) when inside
Four percentile bands: p50 (median), p75, p85, p99
p50: 50% of historical reactions bounded here — typical first stall
p75: 75% of reactions stop within — common reversal area
p85: 85% of reactions don't exceed — strong move territory
p99: only ~1% reach — exhaustion/capitulation signal
Color: Red = high-side bands, Green = low-side bands
Coefficients derived from cross-instrument backtests (NQ, ES, YM, RTY) over 12 years
Universal coefficients hold within ±0.01 across instruments
Multi-TF stacking: when multiple TFs cluster at same price = structurally significant
 
VWAP + SD BANDS
Volume-Weighted Average Price with ±1 and ±2 standard deviation bands
Two anchors: ETH (18:00 ET — full Globex day) or RTH (09:30 ET — cash session only)
VWAP = sum of (typical price × volume) / total volume since anchor
Typical price = (High + Low + Close) / 3
SD bands = volume-weighted standard deviation of typical prices around VWAP
Bands widen in high volatility, tighten in consolidation
 
SINGLE PRINTS
Single-print gaps from prior RTH market profile — act as magnets
Based on 30-minute TPO blocks — price levels with exactly one TPO letter
Tracked over last 10 trading days
Size bucket: Tiny (<0.10%), Small, Normal, Wide (>0.40%)
Tiny zones fill at ~77% — by far strongest predictor
Age decay: D+0 = full base prob, D+2 = 65%, D+5 = 32%, D+10 = 8%
Direction bias: aligning with today's momentum = 1.2× multiplier
Chain position: sequential filling logic when zones stack
Open-inside override: if RTH opened inside an SP zone = 90% fill rating
3,157 zones analyzed across backtest
Backtest: 2014–present across NQ, ES, YM, RTY
 
BIG TRADES
Live tape of large-size prints from CME futures order flow
Aggregated in 2-second windows
Buys (lifts) = green, Sells (hits) = red
Newest 25 prints visible, 36-hour memory
NQ threshold: 50 contracts minimum
Tiers: 50–100 / 100–200 / 200+ contracts (small / medium / large)
~$24M minimum notional across all instruments
Color = aggressor side only, not directional signal

PROBABILITY MODULES — DETAIL
==================================================
 
REACH
Post-breakout extension zones at 5 percentile depths
Activates only when price breaks prior bar's range
Timeframes: 4H, 6H, 8H, 12H
Five bands: p25, p50 (median/retest), p75, p85, p95
p25: first shallow extension — 25% of breaks go this far before open retest
p50: half of breakouts run this far — typical retracement target
p75: 75% of breakouts stop within — common reversal area
p85: strong-move territory — 85% don't exceed
p95: rare extension — only ~5% of breakouts run this deep
Open-retest probability: conditional on current peak extension, bar open position, time elapsed
Coefficients calibrated from 97–221 historical break events per timeframe/slot
Universal across NQ, ES, YM, RTY within std dev under 0.01
 
HOURLY RETRACEMENT
Projects each overnight hourly candle's Open and Mid into RTH session
Hours tracked: 02:00–07:00 ET (Asia hours 20:00–02:00 hidden — weakest edge)
Two levels per candle: Open (actual traded price) and Mid ((high+low)/2)
Hit rate = historical % of days RTH price returned to touch level before cash close
7am Mid hit rate: 94.1% — strongest level
7am Open hit rate: 88.9% — second strongest
Mid levels consistently outperform Open levels at every hour
Conditional decay checkpoints: 9am, 10am, 11am, 12pm, 2pm ET
Levels not hit by 11am lose roughly 15–30 percentage points of remaining probability
P2 ALN pattern adds +5–17 percentage points above baseline
Priority Score (0–100): combines hit rate with distance from price
MAE zones available: median overshoot (inner) and p75 overshoot (outer) per level
ALN Pattern effect:
P1 — London engulfs Asia: variable
P2 — London inside Asia: +5 to +17pp above baseline (strongest)
P3 — London breaks above Asia: upside directional bias
P4 — London breaks below Asia: downside directional bias
Dataset: 3,115 NQ sessions, 10+ years
 
PWH / PWL EDGE
Directional bias based on price position relative to previous week's high/low midpoint
Midpoint = (PWH + PWL) / 2
Above midpoint → PWH touched first: 81–88% of the time
Below midpoint → PWL touched first: 73–78% of the time
Asymmetry reflects natural upward drift in equity index futures
Mid-first collapse: when price crosses midpoint before touching either level, edge collapses to ~50% — does NOT flip direction
After directional week: hit rate jumps to 91–95%
After indecisive week: hit rate drops to 66–71%
Near-extreme positions (Q1/Q4 quartiles): 86–96% hit rates
Mid-range positions (Q2/Q3): only 56–73% hit rates
Most weeks resolve by Tuesday (~76%) — by Wednesday ~89% have touched a level
P(Opposite also touched): typically only 10–15%
Four weighted factors:
  Distance: 35% — price quartile position (strongest factor)
  Previous Week: 30% — was last week directional or indecisive?
  Direction: 20% — above or below midpoint
  Consensus: 15% — do NQ, ES, YM, RTY all agree?
Edge tiers:
  S-TIER: 90%+ — extreme position + directional week + consensus
  A-TIER: 82%+ — strong edge
  B-TIER: 72%+ — moderate edge
  C-TIER: 65%+ — weak edge
  AVOID: below 65% — no reliable edge or mid-first collapse
Dataset: 2,226 inside-range weeks, 12 years
 
WICK FADE
Live session-wick extension probability
Tracks HIGH wick when price < session open, LOW wick when price > session open
Wick % = (running extreme − body edge) / session open × 100
Body edge = max(open, currentClose) for HIGH side, min for LOW side
Hourly-conditional percentile from 16-year NQ backtest (4,204 sessions)
Exceedance probability = historical % of sessions where wick extended further from this percentile at this hour
Score = probability × (100 − percentile) / 100
Color zones:
  Green (≥75%): strong expectation of further extension
  Lime (60–74%)
  Yellow (45–59%)
  Orange (30–44%)
  Red (<30%): extension likely exhausted
Session anchor: 18:00 ET

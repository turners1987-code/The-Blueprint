MODULE 02 The Rules

The Hard Rules

The hard rules are not there to make you money. They are there so that you are still trading on the day your own record is large enough to judge.

Copies this lesson as Markdown to paste into any AI assistant

The Day That Ends The Account

You know the day. The first trade wins. The next one comes a little faster, from a little further away from your level. Then one loses, and the one after that is taken to get it back. By late morning the read you had at 09:30 was right and the day is gone anyway.

An edge, if you have one, shows up in your record over months of trades. A single bad day can take the account before that record exists. These five rules decide how long a bad day can last and how much it can cost. They predict nothing about price; they are simply on, every session.

The order below runs from the mechanical to the psychological. The first four are things a clock, a calendar or a platform setting can enforce. The fifth is the one only you can.

Rule 1: The Trading Window

Trading Window
The hours in which you may place a trade: 06:00 to 16:00 ET, with a focus block of 09:30 to 11:00 ET. Outside the window there is no execution of any kind.

The window has two parts. The full window, 06:00 to 16:00 ET, is when execution is allowed at all. The focus block, 09:30 to 11:00 ET, is the first stretch of the cash session, when your prepared levels meet the most traffic and your attention is freshest. The plan expects most of its trades there. A trade outside the focus block but inside the window is allowed; it is held to the same plan. Nothing new is entered from 15:55 ET (Module 17); the last five minutes are for closing, not opening.

The focus block is not the only way the journal divides the day. Every trade also records the part of the session its entry fell in, so your record can later compare early trades with late ones.

Session Block
The part of the session a trade's entry falls in: early, mid or late. You write the block times once on your PlayBook page and keep them; the journal records the block on every trade.

The PlayBook page is built in Module 23; until you have one, write your three block times once at the top of your journal and leave them alone.

Before 06:00 the overnight hours build levels and take no trades. From 06:00 to 09:30 a trade is allowed only once that day's pre-market sheet is complete (Module 16): no completed sheet, no trade, at any hour. The routine finishes by 09:25 at the latest, and a sheet still incomplete at 09:25 means that session is watched, not traded. The 16:00 end is a convention this method chooses, the cash equity close, not a market fact: futures keep trading after it, and you don't.

The rule: no execution outside 06:00 to 16:00 ET, and no new entries from 15:55 ET (Module 17).

Broke Rule
The journal tag for breaking a hard rule: an entry outside the trading window or from 15:55 ET, a position held through a Tier One release or still open at T−5, a stop widened after entry, a new entry with no post-loss reset, or trading past the kill switch.

Breaking it usually looks like one trade at 16:20 "because the level was right there." What it reveals: the trade came from the screen, not from the plan, and you were still looking for one after the plan had finished. The fix: write the window as the first line of your daily plan, set an alarm for 15:55, when new entries stop, and close the platform at 16:00. Any entry time outside the window, or from 15:55, is tagged Broke Rule in the journal, with no exceptions for winners.

Rule 2: The News Windows

Some scheduled releases move the index futures in a way no level survives. Others are noise you can trade through. The rule sorts them into two tiers and treats each tier one way only.

Tier One Release
CPI, PPI, PCE, NFP, FOMC (the statement and the press conference), and Retail Sales. Be flat 5 minutes before the release; no new entries until 2 minutes after it.
Tier Two Release
Home sales, EIA, consumer sentiment, JOLTS, and Fed speakers. Stay aware of the time, manage your position through it, and never exit a working position because of it.

Around a Tier One number the book thins, spreads widen, and price can jump through your stop rather than trade at it. A stop is only as good as the price it fills at, and in the seconds after a Tier One release that price is anyone's guess. So you are not in. At T−5 you are flat with no resting orders; at T+2 you may enter again, from a level, with a trigger, as on any other bar.

Tier Two is the opposite case. These prints move price, but rarely in a way your stop cannot handle. Exiting a working trade for them is leaving on a feeling, and that is what the next rule is about.

The rule: Tier One, flat at T−5, no entries until T+2. Tier Two, manage through.

Breaking it comes in two forms. Holding through a Tier One number reveals a trade that has become a bet on the release; whatever happens next, the plan did not decide it. Exiting for a Tier Two print reveals a trader who wanted a reason to leave. The fix: each morning, write every Tier One release on the plan with its T−5 and T+2 times beside it, and set an alarm at T−6. Write the Tier Two releases on a separate line headed "manage through". A position open at T−5 on a Tier One day is tagged Broke Rule.

Rule 3: Four Reasons To Flatten By Hand

Manual Flatten
Closing a working position yourself, before its target or its resting stop has done it for you.

A trade that reaches its target, or its planned scale, is the plan working. So is a resting stop that fills. This rule covers the other exits: the ones where your hand closes the trade. There are four reasons to do that, and only four.

  1. Stop hit. Your resting stop normally does this for you. If price has traded through it and the order has not filled, you close the rest by hand. You were wrong; you leave.
  2. Tier One release within 5 minutes. Rule 2, applied to a trade that is still open at T−5.
  3. Thesis broken on a candle close. The invalidation you wrote at entry has happened, judged on the close of the candle you named, not on a tick through it. An idea with no written invalidation was No Setup (Module 08) to begin with.
  4. Session over. It is 16:00 ET. Nothing stays open past the window.

Every exit, by hand or not, gets a mark in the journal for whether it followed the plan.

Execution Mark
Pass or Fail, assigned after the exit: did the exit follow the plan? Pass is a target, the planned scale, a pre-set invalidation, the stop, session end, or a Tier One flatten at T−5. Anything else is Fail. Kept separate from the grade so the grade never reads the outcome.
Too Early
The journal tag for a manual exit taken for any reason other than the four. Its execution mark is Fail, whatever the trade went on to do.

Anything else is Too Early: closing because it "stalled", because you were up and didn't want to give it back, because a red candle looked big, because a Tier Two print was coming. That includes tightening the stop mid-trade to a level you didn't write at entry. Staying in is a separate breach with a separate name: widening a stop is Broke Rule.

The rule: four reasons to flatten by hand. Every other manual exit is logged Too Early.

Breaking it reveals a trader who doesn't trust the stop and target they set when they were calm. The trade had a plan; the exit replaced it with a mood. The fix: every manual exit gets one of the four reasons written in the journal next to it, chosen from a list. If none fits, the tag is Too Early and the mark is Fail. Count your Too Early exits each week; the number is the measure of this rule.

Rule 4: The Kill Switch

This rule is counted in R, so the unit comes first.

R (Risk Unit)
The fixed dollar amount you risk on one trade, chosen before the trade as your base R (Module 14). The stop's distance then sets how many contracts carry that amount. The journal records every outcome in R (2R is twice the risk), so trades of different sizes compare on one scale.
Kill Switch
Your daily max loss: 2R, twice your fixed base risk per trade. When the day's loss reaches it, trading stops until the next session. It is set at the account level so that it fires whether or not you agree with it.

The kill switch is stated in R because R is the only unit this course uses. In the setting itself you will type a dollar figure: two times your base risk. Module 03 shows how ticks and points convert to dollars; Module 14 covers setting the base risk itself. With a fixed base R, two full losing trades bring the day to 2R, and the second loss, with fees, lands just past it; that is where the switch fires.

It is fixed. It is never raised intraday, never "just this once" on a good-looking day, and never adjusted after it fires. There is no ladder of smaller size after a red day, and no mandatory days off after one; the switch ends the session and nothing more. A day away after a hard week is hygiene, not a rule.

Where The Limit Lives

The switch only works if your own hand cannot undo it at 10:40. That means it belongs somewhere outside the window you trade from.

Not in NinjaTrader 8. NT8's Risk window holds templates for simulation accounts only, and its fields are margin and contract limits. There is no daily loss limit in the platform itself. NinjaTrader's own guide sends live accounts to the Client Dashboard.

Source: NinjaTrader, NT8 Help Guide, Understanding Risks and Using the Risk Window, read 2026-10-31.

NinjaTrader Brokerage accounts. In the NinjaTrader Dashboard, open User Profile, then Settings, then the Accounts tab, then Risk Settings, and set the Daily Loss Limit (the Web App's shield icon reaches the same settings). In the same place, turn on "Prevent Changes When Trading is Locked by Risk Settings". With it on, once the limit fires you cannot raise it or switch the lock off yourself for the rest of that trading day. The setting is applied to the account, so it holds on every platform you trade that account through, NT8 included.

Source: NinjaTrader, Risk Settings, support.ninjatrader.com, read 2026-10-31.

Tradovate. Open Application Settings (upper right), choose the Accounts tab, click Risk Settings, turn on the Daily Loss toggle and enter the value. In the same panel, turn on "Lock Risk Settings if Hit". Tradovate's article says that once locked and hit, the settings stay locked until the session ends, and support cannot unlock them. The article does not say whether this differs between live and demo accounts; check yours.

Source: Tradovate, How Do I Set a Daily Loss Limit on My Account and How Do I Lock My Account Risk Settings, tradovate.zendesk.com, read 2026-10-31.

Accounts on Rithmic. Here the loss limit is not yours to set. Rithmic's risk system is configured on the account by whoever provides it (the broker, the clearing firm or the evaluator), and it runs on Rithmic's servers, above the platform. If your account works this way, the account's own daily limit is the backstop, and your 2R becomes a written rule that you enforce by closing the platform when you reach it. That is weaker. Nothing stops your hand but you, and that is exactly what the rule exists to replace. If your venue offers you no lock, write the line anyway and say so in your plan: "2R is written, not enforced."

Source: Rithmic, Risk Management and R | Trader Pro, rithmic.com, read 2026-10-31.

Funded and evaluation accounts. These usually come with a daily loss limit set by the firm, and it is often larger than 2R. Treat it as a backstop, not as your switch. If the firm's number is the only thing that stops you, you are trading to someone else's risk tolerance. Firms change these figures without notice, so read the current one on your firm's own help pages and set your 2R inside it wherever the firm lets you.

Liquidation-Only
The account state after a loss limit fires: open positions are closed, working orders are cancelled, and only orders that reduce a position are accepted until the reset.

Two Things The Limit Does Not Do

It does not cap the loss to the tick. A loss limit is a trigger that sends a market order to close you out. NinjaTrader's own article calls its limits liquidation triggers rather than caps on the loss, because the fill can come back past your number when the market is moving fast. Size so that the second loss of the day lands at 2R through your resting stop, and let the limit catch anything worse.

It may not see an open loss. Venues differ. NinjaTrader Brokerage measures the daily limit on realized profit and loss only, so an open position can run past the number before anything fires. Tradovate's article says it counts realized and open profit and loss, monitored in real time. Both say fees and commissions count. On a realized-only venue, the resting stop on the trade is what bounds the open loss. The limit counts it afterwards.

Sources: NinjaTrader, Risk Settings; Tradovate, How Do I Set a Daily Loss Limit on My Account; both read 2026-10-31.

When It Fires

The mechanism is the platform's job. The behaviour is yours, and it is three steps. Note it once in the journal: the time, and "kill switch". Close the platform. Step away from the desk. Don't open a second account, a phone app or the simulator to keep going. The day is finished, and the next one starts at the reset with the same 2R.

The rule: daily max loss is 2R, fixed, set at the account level with the lock on wherever the venue allows, never raised intraday.

Breaking it looks like raising the limit at 10:40, opening another account, or trading on after a written 2R. What it reveals: the trader is trying to win the day back, and the day has become the unit instead of the trade. The fix: the lock setting, turned on before the session, so the decision is made by a calmer version of you. Where no lock exists, close the platform and leave the room; write the time you closed it in the journal.

Rule 5: The Post-Loss Reset

Post-Loss Reset
The fixed routine run after every losing trade and before any new entry: cancel every working entry order, take a physical step to slow down, then answer three questions aloud. If you can't answer them clearly, you are not ready to trade.

The first four rules are enforced by a clock, a calendar and a setting. This one is enforced by you, which is why it comes last. The reset is a short, rehearsed routine, the same every time, so that it runs on a bad day without having to be remembered.

The cancel. Cancel every working entry order before anything else. A resting limit placed before the loss belongs to the trader you were before it; if the setup is still there after the reset, you place it again.

The physical step. Feet flat on the floor. One long exhale. Then three or four rounds of box breathing.

Box Breathing
Breathing in four equal counts: in for four, hold for four, out for four, hold for four. One round is all four sides of the box.

The three questions, aloud.

  1. What's the chart telling me?
  2. Are any setups forming?
  3. What would need to happen for a setup to present itself?

Answer them out loud, in sentences. "Price is back inside yesterday's range and nothing is at a level; a setup needs acceptance beyond the PD High and a Pause N Go on the pullback" is an answer. "It's going to come back" is not. If you can't answer clearly, you are not ready, and the next trade waits until you can.

The pattern this rule exists to stop has a name.

Re-entry Loop
A run of entries taken one after another without a fresh setup, usually starting after the session's first win: each trade comes faster and from further away from a level than the one before.

The loop usually starts with a win, not a loss. The first trade wins, the next feels easy, and the entries start coming from the middle of the range with no trigger. When one of them loses, the next is taken to get it back. There is no cap on the number of trades in this course. The 2R switch is the cap, the reset is the brake before it, and the journal shows you your trade count per session as data, so you can see the loop for yourself.

The rule: after every loss, the full reset before any new entry.

Breaking it looks like an entry within a minute of a stop-out, or a resting order from before the loss left working and filled during the reset. What it reveals: the trade came from the last trade, not from the chart. The fix: after a loss, cancel every working entry order first; before the next entry, write the answer to the third question in one line in the journal. A blank line means no trade. Rehearse the reset once on a quiet day this week, with no position on, so that the first time you run it is not the day you need it.

Every one of these rules removes a decision from the worst moment to make it. The window, the news times, the four reasons and the 2R limit are decided before the session; the reset is how you get back to the person who decided them.

Worked Case: One Session, Three Rules Firing

Illustration · not a trade record

A composite MNQ session, built to show how the rules interact. It belongs to no one, and the prices are invented for the arithmetic. Risk per trade is 1R. This plan's base R is $100, so the kill switch is typed into the account as $200, with the lock on. Every stop sits 2 points behind the zone's far edge (Module 06), and each trade is sized so its stop is exactly 1R: a 10.00-point stop is 40 ticks, $20 a contract, so 5 contracts. Every trade comes from a branch on the day's sheet, which was complete at 07:40.

07:40 ET. The sheet is finished: levels marked, the news line written with its times (CPI at 08:30, so T−5 is 08:25 and T+2 is 08:32), the idea with its branches, and the systems check done.

07:58 ET. The sheet is complete, so the window is open. Level: the pd-high zone, 21,684.00 to 21,690.00. Read: acceptance (Module 10): first touch at 07:32, then the 5-minute bar ending 07:40 closes at 21,694.00, above the zone, within 15 minutes. Trigger: on the pullback, two 1-minute bars hold inside the zone (pause high 21,689.50) with no close below 21,684.00, and the 07:58 bar closes at 21,692.00, above the pause's high. Long 5 at 21,692.00; stop 21,682.00, 10.00 points (1R). Invalidation: a 5-minute close back below 21,684.00. Target: the prior-week-high zone at 21,790.00, 98.00 points away, well past 2:1.

08:20 ET. CPI is on today's plan at 08:30, so T−5 is 08:25 and T+2 is 08:32. Price is close to the entry. At 08:25 the trader flattens by hand at 21,692.50: +0.50 points, +0.05R before fees. Reason: Tier One release within 5 minutes. Execution mark: Pass. No resting orders go into the number.

08:32 ET. Entries are allowed again. The release has pushed price to 21,740.00 and back; nothing is at a level, so nothing is taken. At 09:20 the trader adds the overnight high the release printed to the sheet, as the on-high zone 21,736.00 to 21,740.00, with a failed-breakout branch, before the 09:25 rehearsal.

09:48 ET. Level: the on-high zone, 21,736.00 to 21,740.00. Read: failed breakout (Module 11): two 1-minute closes above the far edge, 21,741.25 at 09:43 and 21,742.00 at 09:44, then the 09:45 bar closes back below the near edge at 21,735.50. Trigger: two 1-minute bars hold inside the reclaimed zone (pause low 21,736.25) with no close above 21,740.00, and the 09:48 bar closes at 21,732.00, below the pause's low. Short 5 at 21,732.00; stop 21,742.00, 10.00 points (1R). Invalidation: a 5-minute close above 21,740.00. Target: the top of the pd-high zone, 21,690.00, 42.00 points away, 4.2:1.

09:56 ET. The resting stop fills at 21,742.00: −10.00 points, −1R. The trade was built to plan, so it gets no negative tag; execution mark: Pass. The trader runs the reset: cancels every working entry order (none was working), then feet, exhale, four rounds. Aloud: price has traded above the on-high; nothing is forming; a setup would need acceptance above the on-high zone and a Pause N Go on the pullback. They write that line in the journal.

10:35 ET. Read: acceptance. Price came back into the zone at 10:03, and the 5-minute bar ending 10:15 closes at 21,746.00, above 21,740.00, within 15 minutes of that touch. Trigger: the pullback touches the zone at 10:32; the 10:33 and 10:34 bars hold inside it (low 21,737.50, pause high 21,739.75) with no close below 21,736.00, and the 10:35 bar closes at 21,744.00, above the pause's high. Long 5 at 21,744.00; stop 21,734.00, 10.00 points (1R). Invalidation: a 5-minute close back below 21,736.00. Target: the prior-week-high zone at 21,790.00, 46.00 points away, 4.6:1.

10:41 ET. The stop fills at 21,734.00: −10.00 points, −1R; execution mark: Pass. Before fees the day is −1.95R (+0.05R, −1R, −1R); the fees on fifteen contracts' round turns take it just past 2R realized. The account-level limit fires: the account goes liquidation-only, and the risk settings lock until the reset.

10:42 ET. The pull to re-enter is strong; the chart looks as if it is about to go. The trader notes "kill switch, 10:41" in the journal, closes the platform and leaves the desk.

That is where the case stops. What price did afterwards is not part of the lesson, and it is not your money.

Hypothetical illustration. Hypothetical results have inherent limits: they are not actual trading and do not reflect real fills, slippage or the pressure of risk, and no representation is made that any account will achieve similar results.

Notice what the rules did and didn't do. Neither loss was a mistake: both were planned trades, each with its read and its invalidation written before the entry, and the rules never asked for them to win. The rules fired at 08:25, at 09:56 and at 10:41. The one decision that was the trader's alone was at 10:42, and the lock had already made it for them.

Which Rule Is Yours To Fix

Score each line 2, 1 or 0. A 2 means a mechanism enforces it. A 1 means it is written down and you enforce it by hand. A 0 means neither.

  1. Kill switch. 2: set at the account level at 2R with the lock on. 1: written as 2R in your plan, or set without the lock. 0: neither, or the only limit is your firm's.
  2. Window. 2: the window is on the plan, with a 15:55 alarm and the platform closed at 16:00. 1: you know the hours. 0: you have traded outside them in the last two weeks.
  3. News. 2: today's Tier One times are written with T−5 and T+2, with an alarm. 1: you check the calendar but write nothing down. 0: you have held through a Tier One number in the last month.
  4. Manual flatten. 2: every manual exit in your last ten trades carries one of the four reasons or a Too Early tag. 1: some do. 0: none do.
  5. Reset. 2: rehearsed, working entry orders cancelled and the third answer written before every post-loss entry. 1: you do it sometimes. 0: you have never run it.

The threshold: any 0 is fixed before your next session. The kill switch line must score 2 wherever your venue allows it. If yours can't reach 2, it scores 1 and your plan says so in writing. A total under 7 means the rules are a wish, not a system; start with the lowest line.

Before Your Next Session

Apply This Now

Before you trade again, set your daily loss limit at the account level at 2R, with the lock turned on, or confirm that it is already set and locked. If your venue doesn't let you set one, write this line at the top of tomorrow's plan instead: "Kill switch: 2R. At 2R: close the platform." That's the whole assignment.

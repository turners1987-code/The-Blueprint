MODULE 08 The Blueprint

The Three Tiers

The three tiers are not a checklist you run once you already want in: they are three questions asked in a fixed order, and the order is the rule, because each answer only means something inside the one above it.

The Trade You Could Only Half Explain

You saw it go. A long red bar at 09:47, through nothing in particular, and you sold the break of the next bar because the move was real. The stop went "above that high". Two minutes later price came back through your entry, and when you went to write the trade up, you could name the bar. You couldn't name the level, and you couldn't name the price at which the idea was wrong, because there was no idea. There was a bar.

That trade wasn't a bad read. It was a third of a trade: an entry event with nothing above it. This page teaches the whole of it once, in the words every later module uses. Every trade in this course traces up through one chain: idea → setup (location + read) → trigger → construction (level → stop → risk → size → entry).

Three Tiers
The order every trade traces up through: the idea (what should happen, where it is wrong, and how much stands behind it), the setup (the named location and the read of what price is doing there), and the trigger (the price event at that location). Construction follows the three: level, stop, risk, size, entry. A missing tier lowers the grade at entry and carries its honest tag; Module 14 sets what each grade trades.

Seven parts, mechanical first: the three questions in order, then construction, the one sentence that carries all three, and the grade that checks them at entry. The last part is about you rather than the chart: the tier you skip when you want to be in.

Question One: What Is The Idea?

Idea
A conditional statement written before the session: a thesis in if/then form at a named level, the invalidation that ends it, and a conviction grade of strong, moderate or thin. It describes what you will do if price reaches a place, not where price will go.

Show it: "If price accepts below the on-low zone and a Pause N Go fires on the pullback into it, then pd-val. Invalidation: a 5-minute close back above the zone's top edge. Conviction: moderate." Three outputs: a thesis, a price that ends it, and a grade of how much stands behind it. Nothing in it says the market will go down.

The idea is where the bigger picture enters the trade: a catalyst, where price sits against the prior day's range and value, the calendar. Module 09 teaches how those inputs are gathered. What they produce is an idea, never an entry. A higher-frame read is permission to look for a setup; it is not a setup. And several things that feel like ideas aren't one:

  • A day-type label. "Trend day" is a forecast about the whole session. No price can prove it wrong at 09:45.
  • The overnight's direction. The overnight session builds levels for your list. It never supplies a direction.
  • An indicator or another market. A moving average, a crossover or a move in a related market never sets the thesis and is never the lead.
  • A catalyst on its own. News can raise or lower conviction. It doesn't create a level, and it never waives one.
  • Dealer positioning. A gamma regime (Module 04) can weight conviction. A dealer or gamma level is not a location, a target or an if-clause.

The rule: the idea is written before the session, as a conditional at a named level, with its invalidation price and candle timeframe and its conviction. An idea without a named invalidation is No Setup.

No Setup
The journal tag for a trade with no named location and read behind it: a trade taken on feel. An idea without a written invalidation is No Setup, however good it sounds.

Getting it wrong looks like shorting a move that has run a long way "because it has to stop", with no level named and nothing written down. What it reveals: you are betting on a direction, and a bet with no invalidation has no price at which you are wrong, so the stop becomes the only argument you respect. The fix: no order goes in unless it comes from an idea line written on the pre-market sheet (Module 16) before 09:25, with a number and a timeframe in its invalidation. A trade with no line behind it is tagged No Setup in the journal the same session, whatever it made.

Question Two: Where, And What Is Price Doing There?

Setup
The second tier: a named location plus the read of what price is doing there, judged on a closed bar. Price at a level either holds or fails; the read says which. It is recorded in two journal fields, the setup (the read) and the location (the slug).
Location
Where a trade happens: one named level from the pre-marked or in-session list, written as a zone, with the stop behind its far edge. It is recorded by its slug in the journal's location field.

The setup has two halves, and the order matters: where first, then what. The where is a zone from the level list in Module 06: on-low, pd-high, pd-val, an opening-range extreme once its window has closed. The what is a read you can see on a closed bar. Acceptance, a 5-minute close beyond the zone within 15 minutes of the first touch, is the read for Trend Continuation (Module 10). A failure back through a range extreme is Range Rejection (Module 11), which this course teaches as a context read with no entry of its own. A gap, an RTH open outside the prior day's range, gets its own setups (Module 12).

The same footprint event means different things in different places. Aggressive selling at a session high is a read. The same selling halfway between two levels is a print with nothing to stand on. VWAP, moving averages, pivots, Fibonacci levels and round numbers are not locations, and neither is the inside of the initial balance or the middle of a balance range.

Mid-Range
The location tag for any price outside every zone on your list: no named level, so nothing for a stop to sit behind. It exists so that a trade taken there is counted rather than hidden.

The rule: the setup names one slug, written as a zone, and one read seen on a closed bar. If you can't name the slug, the location is mid-range, and the trade is tagged that way.

Trigger Without Location
The named leak of trading an entry event with no level under it: a trigger fired mid-range, with nothing behind the stop. It shows in the journal as location mid-range, and the trade is No Setup.

Getting it wrong is Trigger Without Location: a bar pattern or a burst of movement acted on in the middle of nowhere, usually early in the session when everything moves. What it reveals: you are reacting to movement rather than to a place, and a stop with no structure behind it is a guess about how far noise will go. The fix: every zone on your list carries two platform alerts, one at each edge (Module 17). No alert has sounded, no setup exists, and your hand stays off the order buttons. When you write the sentence before entry, the slug is the first word you write.

Question Three: What Just Happened At The Level?

Trigger
The price event at the level that tells you to enter now, such as Pause N Go, a resting limit after displacement, or a level reclaim on a 5-minute close (Module 13). Order flow can confirm a trigger; it is never the trigger.

Show it with Pause N Go. After the first touch of the zone, at least two consecutive 1-minute bars hold inside it with no 1-minute close through its far edge. That is the pause. Then a 1-minute close beyond the pause's extreme, in the direction of the read: that is the go, and the entry is on that close. Everything in it can be pointed at on a chart after the session.

A resting limit counts as a trigger only after price has displaced away from the level first; a limit placed blind, before anything has happened, has a blank trigger field. Order flow from Module 07, such as failing delta on the pullback, can confirm the trigger, and its sub-form is recorded. It never replaces the price event. An indicator crossing is never a trigger.

The rule: something must happen at the level before entry, and that something is a named price event. A setup with no trigger is no position. When nothing fired, the trigger field is blank, and a blank is data.

Getting it wrong looks like selling the first touch of the zone because the idea was right and the level was right. What it reveals: you trust your idea more than the market's answer to it. A correct idea can still produce no trade, because the level is a question and the trigger is the reply. The fix: the branch names its trigger before the session, and the order is sent only on a closed 1-minute bar that completes that trigger, or as a resting limit the branch named in advance, placed after displacement. If you can't say which trigger just fired, the order isn't sent.

Then Construction: Level, Stop, Risk, Size, Entry

Construction
The arithmetic that turns three answered questions into an order, always in this order: the level (the zone traded against and the target level), the stop (behind the far edge), the risk (the stop distance, and the 2:1 check to the target), the size (from the stop distance and the grade), and the entry. It comes after the three tiers, never before them.

Show it on a short below a zone. Level: the zone being traded against and the target level, both on the sheet before the session. Stop: a resting order behind the zone's far edge, placed with the entry; if your stop floor for that time of day (Module 14) is wider, the stop goes out to the floor. Risk: the distance from entry to stop, in points and ticks. If the target level is less than twice that distance away, there is no trade; 2R is a filter, not a default target. Size: one base R divided by the stop's value per contract, rounded down. A wider stop means fewer contracts, never more risk. Entry: the order, with the intended price written down so the fill can be measured against it.

How the grade at entry sets full or half size is one table, decided once in Module 14. It is not repeated here.

The rule: construction runs in this order, and only after the three questions have answers. The stop is found on the chart; the size is computed from it.

Getting it wrong looks like deciding the contract count first, then putting the stop wherever the loss feels bearable. What it reveals: the money decided the structure instead of the structure deciding the money, so the stop sits inside the noise and the trade is wrong before it starts. The fix: write the stop price before the size, every time, and compute size with a written formula or a position-size calculator from the stop's ticks. If the stop you need looks impossible, the entry has drifted away from the level; go back to question two, not to a tighter stop.

One Sentence Carries All Three

The three tiers fit in one sentence, and the sentence is the test of whether you have them:

"If [read] at [zone], and [trigger] fires, then [target]. Wrong on [invalidation]. Conviction [strong, moderate or thin]."

Each slot belongs to a tier. The read and the zone are the setup. The trigger is the trigger. The target, the invalidation and the conviction are the idea. Module 15 teaches you to write this sentence before the session as a branch of the plan; here it is the thing you say before the order. "If price accepts below the on-low zone, 20,262 to 20,272, and a Pause N Go fires on the pullback, then pd-val at 20,184. Wrong on a 5-minute close above 20,272. Conviction moderate." Anyone with the chart could check every word of it tomorrow.

Two of three is not a lighter version of the trade. A catalyst doesn't stand in for a location, a strong read doesn't stand in for a trigger, and a perfect trigger doesn't stand in for an idea. Either all three are present, or the trade carries the tag that says which one wasn't: No Setup, mid-range, or a blank trigger.

The rule: before every entry, the sentence is complete, with every slot filled by something you can point to on the chart.

Getting it wrong looks like a sentence that trails off: "short here because... it's heavy." What it reveals: the slot you can't fill is the tier you skipped, and you were about to trade without knowing it. The fix: say the sentence aloud, in this order, before the order goes in. The slot where you stall names the missing tier. A stall means no order, and the stall goes on the sheet as a tally mark.

The Grade Checks The Tiers At Entry

Grade (A+ / A / B / C)
The entry-plan score given to every trade at entry, before it resolves, from four checks: the location is a named level, the trigger fired before entry, the stop is behind structure, and the size matches the plan for that stop. A+ is all four with a fill within 2 ticks of the intended price; A is all four; B is three of four with no breach of the stop or size check; C is two or fewer, or any breach of those two. C is no trade.

The four checks are the tiers, read at the moment of entry:

Grade checkTier it checksFails when
1. Location is a named levelSetupThe location is mid-range
2. Trigger present and fired before entryTriggerThe trigger field is blank, or the order went in before the event completed
3. Stop behind structureConstructionThe stop sits inside the noise, short of the far edge
4. Size matches the plan for that stopConstructionSize was chosen first, or sized up on feel
A+: fill within 2 ticks of the intended priceConstruction (entry)The fill drifted away from the level

The idea is checked one step earlier, on the sheet: a trade with no written idea and invalidation behind it is No Setup before the grade is read. Conviction doesn't enter the grade at all; it is recorded so your journal can later sort by it.

The grade reads the plan, never the outcome. A losing A+ is a normal member of the distribution. A winning C is a warning, not a result: the idea can be wrong and the trade still make money, and that is exactly the trade that teaches you the wrong lesson if you grade it by its result.

The rule: the grade is written at entry, before the trade resolves, from the four checks, and it never changes afterwards.

Getting it wrong looks like a journal where the winners are A and the losers are C. What it reveals: you are grading the market, not your process, so every grade cohort ends up meaning "made money" and stops telling you which tier you skipped. The fix: fill the four yes/no boxes within a minute of entry, while the trade is still open, and read the grade off the boxes rather than choosing it. A grade field written after the exit is marked late and left as it was entered.

The Tier You Skip When You Want To Be In

Here is the hard part. The trigger is the only tier with a feeling attached. An idea is a written line and a level is a price on a list, but a trigger is movement, and movement is what makes you want to be in. So the skip almost always runs the same way: the trigger arrives first in your attention, and the trade is built downward from it, with the setup and the idea supplied afterwards, if at all.

It gets worse at the moments it costs most. After a run of winners the criteria loosen without your noticing, because the last few trades seemed to prove you can read it. After a missed move, the next bar that moves looks like the one you missed. Missing a trade costs you nothing. Forcing one costs you the tiers, and then the R.

The rule: you trace a trade up from the idea, never down from the bar. The first words of the sentence are the idea, then the zone and the read, and the trigger comes last.

Getting it wrong looks like a session where you can explain every entry in terms of the bar that triggered it, and none in terms of the level. What it reveals: the wish to be in the market has replaced the plan for being in a trade, and the trigger has become a reason rather than a reply. The fix: a count, not a resolution. Each time your hand reaches for the order before the sentence is finished from the top, put one tally mark on the sheet. The kill switch from Module 02 still caps the day, and the tally tells you, at the end of the week, how often the trigger tried to go first.

A trade is three answers in a fixed order, an idea that names where it is wrong, a named location with a read, and a price event at that location, followed by the arithmetic. A trade missing any one of them is not a smaller trade; it is a different thing, and it carries the tag that says so.

Worked Case: One Short, Traced Up Through The Tiers

Illustration · not a trade record

A composite MNQ short, built to show the tiers in order. It belongs to no one, the prices are invented for the arithmetic, and the case stops when the order is working, before any outcome. Points are index points on the MNQ chart; one tick is 0.25 points.

Context at 09:05: price 20,318.00. Against the PD range (20,140.00 to 20,402.00): inside. Against PD value (20,176.00 to 20,332.00): inside. ON range 20,262.00 to 20,340.00, inside the PD range. No Tier One release today.

Tier 1, the idea, written at 09:10 on the sheet. "If price accepts below the on-low zone (20,262.00 to 20,272.00), meaning a 5-minute close below 20,262.00 within 15 minutes of the first touch, and a Pause N Go fires on the pullback into the zone, then pd-val at 20,184.00, the near edge of its zone (20,176.00 to 20,184.00). Invalidation: a 5-minute close back above 20,272.00. Conviction: moderate; no level sits in the 78-point path to the target, but the zone holds one level only." After acceptance below, price comes back into the zone from underneath, so the far edge for this trade is the top: 20,272.00.

Tier 2, the setup, 10:02 to 10:10. Price trades down into the zone; the near-edge alert sounds and the first touch prints at 10:02. The 10:05 to 10:10 5-minute bar closes at 20,256.25, below 20,262.00, eight minutes after the first touch. Read: accepted below. Setup field: trend-continuation. Location field: on-low.

Tier 3, the trigger, 10:13 to 10:15. Price pulls back up into the zone. The 10:13 and 10:14 1-minute bars both hold inside it: highs 20,268.50 and 20,269.25, closes 20,265.00 and 20,266.25, no 1-minute close above 20,272.00. That is the pause, and its low is 20,263.50. The 10:15 bar closes at 20,261.75, below the pause's low. That is the go. Delta was failing on the pullback, so the confirmation field gets failing-delta; it confirmed the trigger and did not fire it. Trigger field: pause-n-go.

Construction, at 10:15.

  • Level: the on-low zone, far edge 20,272.00; target pd-val, 20,184.00, both chosen at 09:10.
  • Stop: 20,274.00, behind the far edge and above the pause's high of 20,269.25. If your stop floor for this time of day is wider, the stop goes out to the floor and the next two lines are run again on the wider stop.
  • Risk: 20,274.00 − 20,261.75 = 12.25 points, 49 ticks. Room to the target: 20,261.75 − 20,184.00 = 77.75 points. 77.75 ÷ 12.25 is about 6.3, which clears 2:1.
  • Size: one base R divided by the value of 49 ticks per contract, rounded down; the grade at entry decides full or half, from the table in Module 14.
  • Entry: sell on the 10:15 close, intended price 20,261.75, with the resting stop at 20,274.00 sent with it. Fill: 20,261.50, one tick from the intended price.

Grade at entry. Location a named level: yes. Trigger fired before entry: yes. Stop behind structure: yes. Size from the plan for a 49-tick stop: yes. Fill within 2 ticks of the intended price: yes. A+, written at 10:16 with the trade open.

Traced up. The order exists because a trigger fired. The trigger means something because it fired inside a named zone, after price had been accepted below it. The zone was traded because a line written at 09:10 named it, named its target and named the close that would end it. Take away any one of those and the order at 10:15 has no reason left.

The case stops here, with the order working. Whatever price does next, none of the lines above changes, and neither does the grade.

Illustration · not a trade record

The same morning, earlier, traced the same way. A composite; it belongs to no one.

At 09:47 price falls from 20,336.00 to 20,296.00 in four minutes. The 1-minute bar closes below the prior bar's low at 20,296.25, and the archetype who has a trigger and no level sells it. Stop "above that bar", at 20,303.00. Size: the usual number of contracts.

Trace it up. Trigger: a bar breaking a bar is not a named trigger, so the trigger field is blank. Setup: 20,296.00 sits between the pd-vah zone (20,326.00 to 20,332.00) and the on-low zone (20,262.00 to 20,272.00) and belongs to neither, so the location is mid-range. Idea: no line on the sheet names this price, so there is no invalidation, and the trade is No Setup. Construction: a 6.75-point stop with nothing behind it sits inside the noise, and a size chosen before the stop doesn't match the plan for that stop.

Grade at entry: check 1 fails, check 2 fails, and checks 3 and 4 are breached. C, which is no trade. This is Trigger Without Location, written up honestly. Whether it would have made money changes nothing above; a winning C is a warning, not a result. The structure on the chart was the same as in the case above. The order of the questions was not.

Notice that both trades were short, on the same morning, in the same direction. The first was not better because it was right about direction. It was a trade because it answered three questions in order before the arithmetic began.

Which Tier Is Your Skip?

Take your last ten trades. For each one, score a point for each line that is true, tracing up from the entry:

  1. Trigger: a named trigger from Module 13 completed before the order went in. A blank trigger field scores 0.
  2. Setup: the location was a slug from your level list, written as a zone, and the read was seen on a closed bar. mid-range scores 0.
  3. Idea: a line written before the session named this level, with an invalidation price and timeframe and a conviction.
  4. Construction: the stop was behind the far edge and placed with the entry, the target cleared 2:1, and the size came from the stop distance.

4 of 4 is a complete trade. Now count down each line rather than across each trade. The threshold: any line missed on two or more of the ten trades is your skip. If more than one line crosses the threshold, fix the highest tier first (idea, then setup, then trigger, then construction), because each tier only means something inside the one above it. A missed line 3 is fixed on the sheet before the open; a missed line 2 with the zone alerts; a missed line 1 with the closed-bar rule; a missed line 4 by writing the stop before the size. Any trade that missed line 3 was No Setup, and any that missed line 2 was mid-range, whatever either one made.

Before Your Next Session

Apply This Now

Next session, before each entry, say one sentence aloud in tier order: the idea with its invalidation, then the zone and the read, then the trigger that just fired. If the sentence doesn't finish, the order doesn't go in. That's the whole assignment.

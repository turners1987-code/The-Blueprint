MODULE 16 Process

The Pre-Market Routine

Your pre-market routine is not there to tell you what the market will do; it decides, before 09:25, what you will do at each place the market can go.

Copies this lesson as Markdown to paste into any AI assistant

The First Trade You Didn't Plan

You know the morning. You sit down at 09:20, open the chart, and the open is already moving. There are no levels on the screen, or there are yesterday's, half of them stale. A number came out at 08:30 and you only find out when the candle is already a foot long. By 09:34 you are in a trade from the middle of the range, sized on a feeling, with a stop you placed after the fill.

None of that is a reading problem. It is a preparation problem, and preparation is the one part of the day the market has no say in. This page teaches the routine as a fixed sequence of steps that fills one sheet. The routine ends at 09:25 ET, every day. A completed sheet is what clears you to trade. An incomplete one doesn't.

Pre-Market Sheet
The one page you fill before every session: readiness score, context, news, levels as zones, the idea, the systems check, the rehearsal and the time you finished. Finished by 09:25 ET. A completed sheet clears you to trade; an incomplete one doesn't.

There are seven steps, and a clock around them. The steps run mechanical first: context, news, levels, the idea, the systems check, the rehearsal. The last one, readiness, is the only step about you rather than the market, which is why it comes last.

The clock. The routine has a fixed end and a moving start. It ends at 09:25 ET, always. That leaves five minutes of nothing before the cash open: the pen is down, the sheet is beside the screen, and nothing on it changes.

How long it takes depends on where you are. In your first weeks of running it, expect about an hour, so you start around 08:25. Once it is habit, about 30 minutes is normal, and you start around 08:55. The start moves. The finish doesn't. If you are slow today, you start earlier; you never finish later.

The overnight session builds levels for you; that is all it does for the sheet. Execution belongs to the window in Module 02; the sheet is prep, and prep stops at 09:25.

The rule for the clock: the sheet is finished by 09:25 ET. A sheet that is still incomplete at 09:25 doesn't clear you, and that session is watched, not traded.

Getting the clock wrong looks like still marking levels at 09:29, or skipping steps at 09:24 so the sheet "counts". What it reveals: the start time was set by when you woke up, not by how long the routine takes you. The fix: an alarm at 09:25, and a "finished" field on the sheet where you write the actual time. Each week, look at the five finish times. If any is after 09:25, move your start 15 minutes earlier for next week.

Step 1: Context

Context is where price sits before the open, in relation to yesterday and to the overnight. It is location, and only location. It is never a call on direction.

Three things, each written in a word or two:

  1. Price against the prior day's range. Above the PD high, inside the range, or below the PD low.
  2. Price against the prior day's value. Above the PD VAH, inside value, or below the PD VAL.
  3. What the overnight did. The ON high and ON low, and whether the overnight range sits inside the prior day's range or has traded outside it.

Add one line for the calendar, written in nouns and dates (Module 09): Tier One day, OpEx or expiry Friday, quad witching, turn of month, index rebalance close, roll week, exchange holiday or early close. These are notes about how much range and activity to expect. They carry no direction.

Add one line for the room, from Module 09: "ATR [points] · frame [low] to [high]", the 14-day ATR of the RTH session measured from each end of the overnight range. A target outside the frame is marked "outside room" on its idea. ATR sets the room for targets; it never sets a direction or a stop.

Add the regime line from Module 05: balance or imbalance, with its prices and what ends it, such as "Balance, 21,355 to 21,430; ends on acceptance outside either edge."

Then the gap line. A gap in this course has one definition: the RTH open sits outside the prior day's range. You can't know that at 09:00, so you write it as a condition: "gap if the open is above 21,460 or below 21,310; no gap if it is between." You fill in the answer at 09:30. Beside it goes the open line from Module 05, also written as a condition: for each place the open can sit (in value, above value, below value, gap), the targets it puts in play. At 09:30 you circle one word. The open line has no direction field.

Notice what the context step doesn't contain. No "bullish overnight", no "looks heavy", no day-type label. A directional bias written before the open is an opinion with nothing under it, and once it is on the sheet you will read every bar to agree with it. The only directional element on this sheet is the idea in Step 4, and it is conditional.

The rule: context is written as location against named levels. It never carries a direction.

Getting it wrong looks like a context line that reads "strong overnight, expecting higher". What it reveals: you are forecasting, and the forecast will decide your trades before price reaches a level. The fix: the context line allows location words only (above, inside, below, outside) plus the level prices. Anything else written there is struck through before 09:25.

Step 2: News

Before you open a chart, build today's release list with the times. Module 02 sorts releases into two tiers and the sheet prints them exactly that way.

  • Tier One (CPI, PPI, PCE, NFP, FOMC with the press conference, Retail Sales): each one written with its time, its T−5 (flat, no resting orders) and its T+2 (entries allowed again).
  • Tier Two (home sales, EIA, consumer sentiment, JOLTS, Fed speakers): listed with their times on one line headed "manage through". You don't exit a working position for them.

Take the times from the agency that publishes the number, not from memory. The Bureau of Labor Statistics schedules CPI and the Employment Situation (NFP) for 08:30 ET. The EIA's weekly petroleum report has a standard time of 10:30 ET on Wednesday, and it moves in holiday weeks. The Federal Reserve's calendar lists the FOMC meeting dates; it doesn't print the time of day, so read that from the Fed's own announcement for the meeting.

Sources: BLS release schedules for CPI and the Employment Situation; EIA Weekly Petroleum Status Report schedule; Federal Reserve FOMC calendars. All read 2026-10-31. Agencies change schedules; their current page wins.

A Tier One release before 09:30 still goes on the sheet with T−5 and T+2, even with no position open. It tells you why the overnight high may be a spike printed at 08:31, and it keeps the habit identical on every Tier One day.

If a release printed a level before 09:25, such as the overnight high on an 08:31 spike, write that price on the release's line. The number itself, above or below the forecast, is never written on the sheet (Module 09).

The rule: every Tier One release is on the sheet with T−5 and T+2 written, plus any level it printed; every Tier Two release is listed under "manage through".

Getting it wrong looks like checking the calendar and writing nothing down, then meeting a number at 10:00 with an order resting at a level. What it reveals: the calendar was read as information, not turned into times you act at. The fix: for each Tier One time on the sheet, set an alarm at T−6 before you leave the news step. A Tier One line without its alarm set isn't complete.

Step 3: Levels

The levels are the pre-marked locations from the level list in Module 06, and only those: pd-high, pd-low, pd-vah, pd-val, pd-vpoc, settlement, on-high, on-low, on-poc, prior-week-high, prior-week-low, htf-hvn, htf-lvn. Prior-day levels and settlement come from the 09:30 to 16:00 ET session; the prior week runs Sunday 18:00 to Friday 16:00 ET; the ON levels come from the extended hours (Module 03).

VWAP, pivots, moving averages, Fibonacci levels, round numbers and fair value gaps don't go on the level list. They are not locations in this framework, so nothing on the sheet is built from them.

Zone
A level written as a price band with two edges rather than a single line. Price can trade into a zone without breaking it; acceptance through the whole zone is what changes the read.
Far Edge
The edge of a zone on the far side from where price arrives. For a level above current price it is the top of the zone; for a level below, the bottom. The stop goes behind it.

Every level on the sheet is written as a zone with its far edge marked. Because you know where price sits at 09:00, you know which side it will arrive from, so the far edge for the first touch is a lookup, not a judgement: levels above price, the top; levels below price, the bottom. After acceptance through a zone, price comes back to it from the other side, and the far edge your stop goes behind is the other edge (Module 10). Where two or three levels overlap, they become one zone that names every slug in it. A cluster isn't three levels; it is one, with more behind it.

Each row also carries what Module 06 asks of it: the session scope it was built from (RTH, ETH, week or the composite's dates), its column for the session (location or target), and its stop sentence, "the stop goes behind [price] because [what happened there]". Under the rows go three lines. The target line: any poor high or poor low from the prior session, marked "UB" (Module 05). The in-session line: the in-session types you will watch today, each with its ready condition (Module 06). The figures line: any statistic you rely on today, written with its five labels (Module 21), or "none".

The rule: only levels from Module 06's list go on the sheet, each as a zone with its far edge, scope, column and stop sentence written.

Getting it wrong looks like a single price for a level, or a VWAP line that has crept onto the list. What it reveals: the stop will be placed on the line, inside the noise, or the location will be something the framework doesn't count. The fix: every level row has seven fields: slug, near edge, far edge, side of price, scope, column and stop sentence. A row with a blank field, or a name that isn't a slug, is deleted before you move on.

Step 4: The Idea

The idea is the whole point of the sheet. It is written in the same form Module 15 uses for every plan: a thesis, an invalidation and a conviction, tied to named levels.

Idea
A conditional statement written before the session: a thesis in if/then form at a named level, the invalidation that ends it, and a conviction grade of strong, moderate or thin. It describes what you will do if price reaches a place, not where price will go.
  • Thesis. "If price does X at level A and trigger T fires, then level B is the target." X is a behaviour you can see at the level, such as acceptance (Module 10) or the failed breakdown/breakout read (Module 11). T is a named trigger from Module 13; a thesis with no trigger is not written.
  • Invalidation. The named price, judged on a candle close, at which the thesis is wrong. It sits at or behind a zone's far edge.
  • Conviction. Strong, moderate or thin, counted from the three checks in Module 09 (zone, path, room and clock; three points strong, two moderate, one or none thin), with a reason clause naming the check that failed. It is written so the journal can compare it with how the session went. Conviction doesn't change your size. Size comes from the grade, in one table (Module 14).

You can write a second idea, usually the mirror at the other side of value. Then write the nothing case: the zone where neither idea applies and there is no trade. "Between the PD VAL and PD VAH with neither PD extreme tested: no trade" is a complete line, and often the most useful one on the sheet.

No Setup
The journal tag for a trade with no named location and read behind it: a trade taken on feel. An idea without a written invalidation is No Setup, however good it sounds.

The rule: the idea is if/then at named levels with a named trigger, a written invalidation and a counted conviction. No written invalidation means No Setup.

Getting it wrong looks like "looking for longs today" or a thesis with no price where it stops being true. What it reveals: you have a wish about direction, and with no invalidation you will hold it after the market has said no. The fix: an idea line is accepted only if it contains the word "if", a slug, a named trigger, the word "then", and an invalidation price. Missing any of the five, the line is crossed out, and a trade taken off it is tagged No Setup.

Step 5: The Systems Check

Six boxes. Each is ticked only after you have looked at the screen, not from memory.

  1. Data live. The platform shows connected, and the last trade time on your chart matches the clock.
  2. 2R limit set. Your daily loss limit is set at the account at 2R with the lock on. Where your venue gives you no lock, the line on the sheet reads "2R is written, not enforced" (Module 02).
  3. Chart templates. The chart's session template ends at 16:00 ET, both the RTH template and the full-session one. If you use a footprint, its saved template's name and settings are written on the sheet, such as "FP 5-15 BidAsk" (Module 07).
  4. Contract month. The symbol in the order ticket is the contract you mean to trade. In roll week, read the month character by character (Module 03).
  5. Order template matches the plan. A bracket with a resting stop and a target; automatic breakeven off; no trail; nothing in the template that would move your stop for you.
  6. Alerts set. Two alerts on every zone, one at the near edge and one at the far edge, with the sound on (Module 17).

The rule: all six boxes ticked from the screen before 09:25.

Getting it wrong looks like finding out at 09:41 that the order went to the expiring month, or that the template moved your stop to breakeven. What it reveals: you trusted yesterday's setup to still be today's. The fix: the six boxes on the sheet, in this order, ticked only with the setting on the screen in front of you. A box you can't tick is fixed before 09:25 or the sheet is incomplete.

Step 6: The Rehearsal

Rehearsal
Running the written plan once, aloud, before the open: the idea at its level, the trigger, the stop, the stop filling, the post-loss reset and the kill switch. A practised step, the same every day, not a hope about the session.

Read the idea aloud as a sequence of things that happen and what you do at each. Price reaches the zone. You wait for the trigger. You enter, with the stop behind the far edge. Then the part people skip: the stop fills, the trade is −1R, and you run the post-loss reset from Module 02 word for word: cancel every working entry order, take the physical step, then answer its three questions aloud. Then the second loss: 2R, the limit fires, you write the time and close the platform.

Rehearse the nothing case too: price sits in the middle and you do nothing, for an hour if that is what it does. Doing nothing is the hardest branch to carry out live, so it is the one most worth rehearsing.

The rule: run the plan once, aloud, including the loss path and the nothing case, before 09:25.

Getting it wrong looks like ticking the box because you "know the plan". What it reveals: you have rehearsed the entry and not the exit, so the first time you practise the stop-out is when it costs you. The fix: the rehearsal box has three short fields you fill after saying them: the entry line, the stop line, the reset line. Three blanks, no tick.

Step 7: Readiness

The last step is the only one about you. Before you finish, score your state on one line, 0 to 2, and write it at the top of the sheet.

Readiness Score
A 0 to 2 score written at the top of the pre-market sheet. 2: curious about what the market will do at your levels. 1: neutral. 0: needing something from the market today. It is recorded as data for the journal, not used as a gate.

The question behind it is simple. Do you want to see what happens at your levels, or do you need it to go a certain way? Needing shows up as a reason outside the chart: the account is down, an evaluation is close to its target, last week went badly, the rent is due. Write the score and one line saying why.

The score is a recorded field. It is not a gate. A 0 doesn't stop you trading, and a 2 doesn't clear you. The completed sheet is what clears you. The score's job is later, in the journal, where you can set your readiness scores beside your grades and your rule breaks and see whether your 0 days look different from your 2 days. That is your record's answer, not a rule from this page.

The rule: a readiness score of 0, 1 or 2, with one line of reason, written at the top of the sheet every session.

Getting it wrong comes in two forms. Writing 2 every day reveals a field that has turned into a formality, so the journal will have nothing to compare. Leaving it blank on a bad morning reveals the opposite: the days you most need on the record are the ones you hide. The fix: print the three definitions next to the field and pick the one sentence that matches, word for word. Score it before you look at the account balance. A blank score means the sheet isn't complete.

The routine produces one thing: a written idea at named levels, with its invalidation, finished by 09:25. A completed sheet is what clears you to trade; it never needs to know where price will go.

Worked Case: The Sheet, Blank And Filled

Copy this into whatever you write on, paper or a note. One copy per session. Every field is filled, or the sheet isn't complete.

The Pre-Market Sheet
  1. Readiness: 0 / 1 / 2, and one line why. (2 curious, 1 neutral, 0 needing something from the market.)
  2. Date · contract · finished at: date, contract month, time the sheet was finished (09:25 at the latest).
  3. Calendar: nouns and dates only: Tier One day; OpEx or expiry Friday; quad witching; turn of month; index rebalance close; roll week; holiday or early close.
  4. Room: ATR [points] · frame [low] to [high].
  5. Context: price vs PD range (above / inside / below); price vs PD value (above / inside / below); ON range vs PD range (inside / outside); ON high, ON low.
  6. Regime: balance or imbalance, its prices, and what ends it.
  7. Gap and open: gap if the open is above [PD high] or below [PD low]; the targets in play for each open (in value, above value, below value, gap); one word circled at 09:30.
  8. Tier One: release, time, T−5, T+2, alarm set at T−6, any level it printed.
  9. Tier Two, manage through: release and time, one line.
  10. Levels: one row per level: slug, near edge, far edge, above or below price, scope, column (location or target), stop sentence. Overlapping levels share one row.
  11. Target line: poor highs and lows marked "UB", or "none".
  12. In-session line: the in-session types watched today, each with its ready condition.
  13. Figures: any statistic relied on today, with its five labels, or "none".
  14. Idea 1: if [behaviour] at [slug, zone] and [trigger] fires, then [target slug]. Invalidation: [price, on a candle close]. Conviction: strong / moderate / thin, counted from the three checks, with the failed check named.
  15. Idea 2 (optional): same parts.
  16. Nothing case: the zone where no idea applies: no trade.
  17. Systems: data live; 2R limit set and locked (or "written, not enforced"); session templates end 16:00 ET, footprint template written; contract month checked; order template matches the plan; alerts: two per zone, set, sound on.
  18. Rehearsal: entry line, stop line, reset line, each spoken aloud.

The sheet carries no day-type label, no directional call and no target for the day's profit. Everything on it is a location, a time, a condition or a setting.

The sheet is complete at 09:21, so you are cleared to trade. The pen goes down. What happens after 09:30 belongs to Module 17, not to this sheet.

Notice what the sheet didn't need. It has no view on whether CPI was good or bad, no guess at the day's range and no direction. The 08:31 spike shows up only as where the ON high sits. Both ideas are conditions, each with a trigger and a price that ends it, and the busiest line on the sheet is the one that says "no trade".

Is Your Sheet Complete?

Take your last five sessions. For each one, score one point for every line that was on paper before 09:25:

  1. Finish time written, and 09:25 or earlier.
  2. Context written as location only, with no direction word; the calendar line in nouns; the room line, "ATR [points] · frame [low] to [high]"; the regime and open lines.
  3. News: every Tier One release with T−5, T+2 and any level it printed; Tier Two listed.
  4. Levels: every row a slug from Module 06's list, with both edges, the far edge, scope, column and stop sentence; the target, in-session and figures lines filled.
  5. Idea: if/then at a slug, with a named trigger, an invalidation price and a conviction counted from the three checks.
  6. Systems: all six boxes ticked, alerts included.
  7. Rehearsal and readiness: the three rehearsal lines filled and a readiness score written.

7 of 7 is a complete sheet. Anything less is incomplete, and a session traded off an incomplete sheet is the first thing to fix. The threshold: if two or more of your five sheets scored under 7, or any of them missed line 5, the routine isn't running yet. Find the line you miss most often and fix that one first; the idea line comes before all others.

Before Your Next Session

Apply This Now

Tonight, copy The Sheet onto one page and fill in a single line for tomorrow: the news line, with every Tier One release, its time, its T−5 and its T+2, from the agency's own schedule. That's the whole assignment.

Where It Goes Next

The idea on the sheet is the plan template from Module 15, Writing The Plan; the sheet is where it gets written each morning. Module 17, The Session And The Close, picks up at 09:30: alerts set at the levels on this sheet, the plan as the only input during the session, and the end-of-day routine at the close.

The readiness score and the finish time stay on the sheet as data. In the weekly review in Module 18, Journaling & Review, you set the week's sheets beside your grades and execution marks, which is where a pattern in your 0 days would show up, if your record has one.

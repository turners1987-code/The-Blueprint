MODULE 12 The Blueprint

The Gap Setups

A gap is not a trade and not a direction; it is one fact about where the open sat, and the only thing it changes on your plan is where you aim.

Copies this lesson as Markdown to paste into any AI assistant

The Gap That Had To Fill

You know the morning. The open prints a hundred points above yesterday's high. Gaps fill; you have heard it a hundred times. So at 09:31 you sell the open, with a stop above the first bar. At 09:36 the stop goes. At 09:44 you sell again, and that stop goes too. At 12:40 price trades all the way down to yesterday's close without you, and the lesson you take from the day is that you were early.

You weren't early. You had a target and no trade: no level under the entry, no event that said now, and a target that wasn't even the right one. This page teaches the gap setups as the rest of the course teaches every setup: a definition that a single price answers, a target that is a level, and an entry that needs a level and a trigger like any other trade.

There are five parts, mechanical first: what counts as a gap, where Gap Fill aims, what the trade under it needs, and how Gap N Go goes into your register instead of your plan. The last part is about you rather than the chart: the story every gap tells, and why it is loudest on the mornings it costs the most.

What Counts As A Gap

Every trade in this course traces one chain: idea → setup (location + read) → trigger → construction (level → stop → risk → size → entry). A gap belongs to the setup tier. It changes where price starts the day and what the setup aims at. It doesn't change anything else in the chain.

Gap
An RTH open outside the prior day's range: above the PD High or below the PD Low. Nothing else is a gap in this course. The Gap Setups (Module 12) target the PD range edge, not the prior close.

Show it with one prior session: PD range 21,520 to 21,690, prior close 21,671. Four possible opens:

09:30 openAgainst the PD rangeIn this course
21,742Above the PD High (21,690)Gap up
21,682Above the prior close, inside the rangeNot a gap
21,640Inside the range, after an overnight high at 21,760Not a gap; the overnight built the on-high, nothing more
21,505Below the PD Low (21,520)Gap down

The second row is the one that catches people. Many charting tools, and TradingStats' own Gap Fill study, measure a gap from the prior close, so an open of 21,682 counts there as a gap up. Their fill rates count a different event on a different set of sessions, and none of them is used in this course. Other things carry the word too. A fair value gap is a stretch of candles that didn't overlap, and it is not a location (Module 06). A thin stretch of a profile is a low volume node or a single print (Module 05). The weekend break is no session; Sunday's reopen starts ETH, which builds levels for your list and nothing else. None of these is a gap here.

The rule: a gap is answered once, at the 09:30 print, from two prices written before the session. Above the PD High is a gap up, below the PD Low is a gap down, and anything between is no gap.

Getting it wrong looks like calling the 21,682 open a gap and aiming the trade at the prior close. What it reveals: you are measuring from the close, so you are borrowing a story, and a set of numbers, that belong to a different event from the one on your chart. The fix: the gap line on the Module 16 sheet, written before 09:25: "gap if the open is above [PD High] or below [PD Low]; no gap between". At 09:30 you write one word beside it: up, down or no. If the word is no, every gap branch on the plan is struck through.

Gap Fill Aims At The PD Range Edge

Gap Fill
The gap setup that aims back at the prior day's range: after a gap up the target is the PD High, after a gap down the PD Low, never the prior close. It sets the target and the context; a trade on a gap morning is an ordinary setup (Module 10 or 11) at a named level, with a trigger from Module 13.

Show it: the open prints 21,792, above a PD High of 21,690. The pd-high zone is 21,684 to 21,690, and price would arrive at it from above, so the edge it reaches first is 21,690. That is the target: 102 points below the open. The prior close, 21,671, sits another 19 points further down, inside the prior session's range. It is not on the level list in Module 06, and it is not the target. After a gap down the picture flips: the target is the pd-low zone's edge that price reaches first from below, the PD Low itself.

A target is a level a trade aims at, written before entry. It is never a reason to enter. On a gap-morning branch it is set before the session, as a slug and a price, and it isn't moved once the trade is on. The one planned scale applies as on any other trade: half off at 2R, the runner to the level.

The rule: after a gap up, Gap Fill's target is the PD High; after a gap down, the PD Low. The price is the edge of that zone that price reaches first. Never the prior close.

The figure behind that rule comes from TradingStats' NQ Session Open data, which sorts each session by whether the RTH open sat above, inside or below the prior session's range. A touch there means the line traded at any point in that session's RTH, from the 09:30 open to the 16:00 close. On the 811 sessions that opened above it, price touched the prior session high on 64.1% of them and the prior close on 46.7% (TradingStats Session Open data, 3,163 sessions, source of truth updated 2026-06-08; n=811). On the 473 sessions that opened below it, price touched the prior session low on 67.0% and the prior close on 45.9% (same dataset and date; n=473). A touch rate is not expectancy: a level reached often still loses money if the entry sits in the wrong place (Module 21).

Touch Rate
The share of sessions in which price reached a line at any point in a stated window, counted from a stated start such as the open. It says nothing about the order of events, where an entry sat, or whether a stop was hit first.

Read the two pairs as what they are. Across those sessions, the range edge was reached more often than the close. They don't say which way price went first, when the touch came, or how far it travelled against an entry on the way. They aim the target. They never tell you to enter, and they never give the session a direction.

Getting it wrong looks like a gap branch whose target slot says "gap fill" or "yesterday's close". What it reveals: you are trading a saying rather than a level, and the saying picks the further, less-visited price of the two. The fix: the target slot on a gap branch accepts one of two slugs, pd-high or pd-low, followed by its price. Any other word in that slot and the branch is struck through before 09:25.

The Entry Needs A Level And A Trigger

Gap Fill gives you a target and a context. It does not give you an entry. The target says where. It doesn't say where from, or when. Those come from the same things every trade in this course needs: an ordinary setup, either acceptance (Module 10) or a failed breakdown or breakout with its reclaim (Module 11), at a zone from the level list, and a trigger that fires inside it. On a gap morning that trade simply aims at the PD range edge.

Show it on the same morning. The open, 21,792, is not a location. It is where the first RTH trade printed; nothing happened there before it, so nothing sits behind a stop placed above it. It fails the stop-structure test in Module 06. The zones that can carry a trade toward the PD range edge sit between the open and the target:

  • Pre-marked zones the gap left in the way. After a gap up, an on-low that sits above the PD High; after a gap down, an on-high below the PD Low.
  • In-session levels once they have formed. The opening range low or high once its window has closed (this course's examples use 09:30 to 09:45 ET), or a defended pullback high or low.

At the zone, the read is the setup's own, judged on a closed bar exactly as its page teaches it: acceptance through the zone toward the prior range, a 5-minute close beyond it within 15 minutes of the first touch (Module 10), or two 1-minute closes beyond the zone and a reclaim back through it (Module 11). Then the trigger the setup's page allows, one of the price events in Module 13, such as Pause N Go or, after acceptance, a 9 EMA pullback; Module 13 defines each one. Order flow can confirm the trigger (Module 07). It never replaces it.

The journal records that trade as the ordinary setup it is. The setup field holds the read you used, trend-continuation or range-rejection; the gap goes in as context, noted in the tags as "gap up" or "gap down" with the PD range edge as the target. A gap is not the setup field's answer, because the gap never supplied the read.

The geometry is checked at the trigger. Module 14 requires the trade to clear 2:1 to a target that is a level, with the stop behind the far edge; here that target is the PD range edge. A zone that sits too close to the PD range edge for 2:1 is no trade, however clean the trigger. The opening minutes carry no clock ban. Their structure is wider, which means a wider stop behind the far edge, and a wider stop means a smaller size (Module 14).

The rule: a branch on a gap morning is an ordinary setup: it names a zone between the open and the PD range edge, the read from Module 10 or Module 11 on a closed bar, and a Module 13 trigger that must fire in that zone, aimed at the PD range edge. With no zone between the open and the target, there is no branch toward the PD range edge, and the gap goes in the nothing case.

Getting it wrong looks like a sell at the open, or on the first bar's break, with the stop above the first bar. What it reveals: the target is doing the job of the location. The stop sits in the opening's noise with nothing behind it, and in the journal the trade reads location mid-range, trigger none: No Setup. The fix: the open price never goes on your level list, and no order is armed on a gap morning until a near-edge alert on a named zone has fired (Module 17). Until an alert fires, there is nothing to place.

Gap N Go Goes In Your Register, Not Your Plan

Gap N Go
The idea that after a gap, price keeps travelling away from the prior day's range. This course gives it no read, no trigger and no entry. It sits in your parking lot and leaves it only as a locked, dated entry in your own register (Modules 22 and 23).

Show it: the open prints above the range, the first pullback holds, and price extends higher. Traders talk about this pattern every gap morning, and it is a fair idea to have. This page publishes no way to trade it. If you want to trade it, it has to be written first as an entry in your register, the way Module 22 teaches, and then only your own record can say anything about it.

Register Entry
One numbered, dated page in your own register for one idea: the rule as locked before any data was opened, the null it is tested against, its status, its n (live and sim shown apart), and its reopen condition if it failed. A change to the rule is a new entry, never an edit.

A Gap N Go entry has every slot that a plan branch has, filled in before you open a single chart of past gap days:

SlotWhat goes in it
ConditionThe gap, exactly as this page defines it: the RTH open outside the prior day's range. Not the prior close.
LocationOne slug from the level list, written as a zone.
ReadWhat price must do at that zone, on a closed bar, by a stated time.
TriggerOne trigger from Module 13, named.
StopBehind the zone's far edge: structure, not a number of points.
TargetA level from the list.
WindowThe clock times inside which it may fire.
Costs and nullCommission and slippage per trade, and the block-shuffled null it has to beat.
LockedThe date the paragraph was written, before any data was opened.

Module 22 carries the rest of the protocol. Any setting you sweep is swept on a training window only. A filter that skips some sessions makes it a new entry. A backtest result is an upper bound on what you could do live, and sim trades are counted apart from live ones, never pooled.

The rule: Gap N Go stays in your parking lot until it is a locked, dated register entry. Until then, a gap morning with no triggered branch is the nothing case: no trade.

Getting it wrong looks like "gap and go" trades taken on the mornings it "looked strong", each with a slightly different entry and hold rule, chosen as the session went. What it reveals: the definition moves with the outcome, so no count can ever form and every result gets explained afterwards. The fix: write the register paragraph and date it before you open any data. A word changed after that date is a new entry number; the old entry stays as it was.

Every Gap Tells A Story

"It has to fill." "It's going." Both sentences arrive in the first minute of a gap morning, before a single bar has closed, and both are forecasts about the whole session. Neither one is a level. The bigger the gap, the louder the story, and the touch rates make it louder still: 64.1% (TradingStats, n=811) sounds like a promise. It isn't. It counts sessions, including the ones where your stop went first and the target came hours later without you.

This is the hard part, because a gap feels like information about direction. It is information about where the open sat. Everything else on a gap morning is built the way it is built on any other morning: branches at zones, an invalidation price on each, a conviction counted from Module 09's three checks with a one-clause reason, and a nothing case. Conviction never changes size. Size comes from the grade, in one table, in Module 14.

The rule: the gap adds a target to the plan. It never adds a direction, and the gap's size changes only the distance to the target, which is arithmetic for the 2:1 check.

Getting it wrong looks like a second sell, a minute after the first stop, with no new trigger, because "it still has to fill". What it reveals: the belief in the fill is doing the trigger's job, and that is how the re-entry loop starts. The fix: a re-entry is the same setup plus a fresh trigger. Before any second entry toward the PD range edge, write that trigger's price condition in the trade note, such as "1-minute close below 21,748.50". No line, no order. The 2R kill switch in Module 02 is the only cap, and it is set at the account level, not in your head.

A gap tells you where the prior session's range was left behind, not which way the session goes. Gap Fill aims at the PD range edge, never the prior close, and only an ordinary setup at a named level, with a trigger that fired, decides whether there is a trade at all.

Gap-morning branch, written invalidation first. Invalidation: a 5-minute close back above 21,756. Setup and read: trend-continuation, acceptance per Module 10. Level and trigger: if the open is a gap up, and price accepts below the on-low zone (21,748 to 21,756), meaning a 5-minute close below 21,748 within 15 minutes of the first touch, and a Pause N Go fires on the first pullback into the zone. Target: pd-high, 21,690, the PD range edge Gap Fill sets. Conviction: moderate, because the zone holds one level only; the path to the target is clear, and the target sits inside the room frame with no Tier One release in the 09:30 to 11:00 window.

Nothing case: no gap, or a gap down: this branch is struck, and no gap-down branch is written. Above the on-low zone, without acceptance below it: no trade.

09:30. The open prints 21,792. Gap line: up. The branch is live, and its target is 102 points below the open.

09:58. First touch of the on-low zone, a low of 21,754.

10:05. The 5-minute bar closes at 21,739, below 21,748, seven minutes after the first touch. That is acceptance below the zone. The branch now waits for the pullback.

10:11. Price trades back up into the zone from below. Price left the zone at 10:05, so this touch starts test 2 (Module 10). On the pullback, price arrives from below, so the far edge is the top, 21,756:

The trigger. Two consecutive 1-minute bars held inside the zone with no close through its far edge; the 10:14 bar closed below the pause's low. Pause N Go fired. Entry: 21,746.00, on that close.

Construction. The stop rests at 21,758.00, two points behind the far edge of 21,756, placed with the entry: 12 points, 48 ticks of risk. The target, 21,690, is 56 points away: about 4.67R, so the trade clears 2:1. The planned scale is half off at 2R, 21,722.00, with the runner to 21,690. The size comes from the 12-point stop and the grade, in Module 14. Journal fields: setup trend-continuation, location on-low, test number 2, trigger pause-n-go, confirmation none, the session block, and "gap up" in the tags as context.

10:38. Price trades 21,722. Half off: +2R on that half, 24 points.

11:26. Price reaches 21,690. The runner exits at the target: about +4.67R on that half, 56 points. On the whole position, about +3.3R before costs. The stop on the runner stayed at 21,758 throughout; there is no breakeven move. Execution mark: Pass.

After the exit, price went on through the PD High and through the prior close, to 21,668 at 12:52, then back up to 21,701 by 14:00. None of that belongs to the trade. After the exit is not your money.

Illustration · not a trade record

The same morning, the same plan, the same levels. A composite; it belongs to no one.

09:30. The open prints 21,792. Gap line: up. 10:05. The 5-minute bar closes at 21,739, below 21,748, seven minutes after the first touch. Acceptance below the zone, as before.

10:06 to 10:40. Price never comes back. The highest 1-minute bar after 10:05 reaches 21,744, four points short of the zone. There is no pullback into the zone, so there is no pause and no resumption. No trigger.

10:41. Price is at 21,712, already 36 points below the zone, and the chart looks obvious. Run the arithmetic on a sell here. With the stop behind the far edge at 21,758, the risk is 46 points to a target 22 points away: about 0.48 to 1, which fails 2:1. With a stop anywhere nearer, nothing sits behind it: location mid-range, trigger none, No Setup.

11:15. Price reaches 21,690. No trade.

This session would count among the gap-up sessions where the PD High was touched. It paid nothing, because there was nothing to take. The plan said where and when, the market didn't offer the when, and the result of the morning is zero, recorded as a branch that never triggered.

Notice what stayed the same across both mornings: the gap, the target, the zone and the touch. Everything that decided the trade was one event at one place, a pause inside the on-low zone and a close below it. The target was reached both times. Only one of those mornings had a trade in it.

Is Your Gap Morning A Plan?

Take your last five gap mornings, traded or not. Score each one, a point per line that is true:

  1. Gap line: written before 09:25 with the PD High and PD Low prices, and answered at 09:30 with one word.
  2. Target: the gap branch's target slot named pd-high or pd-low with its price, not the prior close.
  3. Zone: the branch named a zone between the open and the target.
  4. Trigger: any entry came on a Module 13 trigger that fired in that zone, not on the open or the first bar.
  5. Geometry: the 2:1 check against the PD range edge target was done at the trigger, with the stop behind the far edge.
  6. Gap N Go: no trade in the gap's direction unless it matched a locked, dated entry in your register.
  7. Re-entry: every second entry toward the PD range edge had its own fresh trigger written in the trade note first.

Lines 3 and 4 override the score. A trade that missed either one was No Setup, however the rest scored. The threshold: if two or more of your five mornings missed line 3 or line 4, fix those first: no order is armed on a gap morning until a near-edge alert on a named zone has fired. Otherwise, 7 of 7 is a gap plan. If two or more mornings scored under 7, find the line you missed most often and fix that one before the next gap morning.

Before Your Next Session

Apply This Now

Before tomorrow's open, write the gap line on your sheet with both PD prices, and under it one line: "Gap up: target pd-high, [price]. Gap down: target pd-low, [price]." Two lines. That's the whole assignment.

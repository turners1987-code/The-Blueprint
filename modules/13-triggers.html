MODULE 13 The Blueprint

Triggers

A trigger is not your reason to trade; it is the market's reply to a question you already asked at a level, and until price has replied, you have no entry, however right the idea is.

Copies this lesson as Markdown to paste into any AI assistant

Too Early, Then Too Late

You know both halves of this. Price comes down to the zone you marked last night. The idea is right and the level is right, so you sell the first touch, with a stop a few points above it. Price spikes through, takes the stop, and then falls exactly where you said it would, without you.

The next time you wait. Price reaches the zone, turns, and leaves. Twenty points later you can't stand it and buy at market. Now the only stop with structure behind it is so far away that the trade makes no sense, so you put it somewhere closer, inside nothing, and the first ordinary pullback takes it. One morning you entered before anything happened. The other you entered long after it did. Neither entry was on a trigger.

This page is the trigger tier. There are seven parts, mechanical first: the governing rule, the four price events the journal can record (Pause N Go, the resting limit after displacement, the 9 EMA pullback and the level reclaim), order flow as confirmation, and the fill. The last part is about you rather than the chart, which is why it comes last.

Something Must Happen At The Level

Every trade in this course traces one chain: idea → setup (location + read) → trigger → construction (level → stop → risk → size → entry). The setup says where and what: a named zone and the read of what price is doing there. The trigger says when. Either one without the other is no trade.

Trigger
The price event at the level that tells you to enter now, such as Pause N Go, a resting limit after displacement, or a level reclaim on a 5-minute close (Module 13). Order flow can confirm a trigger; it is never the trigger.

The governing rule has two halves. Something must happen at the level before entry, and that something is a price event: a bar close or a fill you can point to on the chart tomorrow. Order flow can confirm the event. It never replaces it. The journal's trigger field takes one of four values, pause-n-go, resting-limit, ema-pullback or level-reclaim, or the value none.

No Trigger
The value none in the journal's trigger field: nothing fired at the level before entry. It is data, not a gap in the record; it is how entries taken without a trigger get counted.
Trigger Without Location
The named leak of trading an entry event with no level under it: a trigger fired mid-range, with nothing behind the stop. It shows in the journal as location mid-range, and the trade is No Setup.

Show it on a pd-low zone of 24,104.00 to 24,110.00, with a short branch written for it. Price arrives at 10:06 and prints 24,109.75. A sell at that print has a location and a read waiting to happen, and no trigger: the journal says none, and the grade loses its second check, that a trigger fired before entry (Module 14). The same price event in the middle of the range, 40 points from any zone, is the mirror leak: a trigger with no location.

The rule also covers the second entry. If the first trade at a zone is stopped out and the branch's invalidation has not printed, the setup still stands, and you may enter again. A re-entry needs its own trigger, fired after the first trade closed.

Re-entry
A second entry at the same setup after the first trade has closed. It is the same setup plus a fresh trigger, recorded like any other trade. There is no count cap; the kill switch is the cap.

The rule: no entry without a named price event at a named zone. Every entry, the first and every re-entry, carries its own trigger in the journal, or none.

Getting it wrong looks like a trigger field filled in on the evening review, from memory, with whatever trigger the chart now seems to show. What it reveals: the trigger is being treated as a story about the trade instead of a condition for it, so your record will show triggers on trades that never had one. The fix: the trigger slug and its price condition go in the trade note before the order is armed: "pause-n-go, 1-minute close below 24,104.25". If you can't write that line, the order isn't sent. If you send it anyway, the field says none.

Pause N Go

Pause N Go
Pause: after the first touch of a level, at least two consecutive 1-minute bars hold inside the zone with no 1-minute close through its far edge. Resumption: a 1-minute close beyond the pause's extreme in the direction of the read; entry on that close; stop behind the far edge. The session block (early / mid / late) is recorded on every trade.
Far Edge
The edge of a zone on the far side from where price arrives. For a level above current price it is the top of the zone; for a level below, the bottom. The stop goes behind it.

Show it on an on-high zone of 21,664.00 to 21,668.00. Price has been accepted above it (Module 10) and pulls back. It touches 21,668.00, the near edge, at 10:48. The 10:49 bar (high 21,669.50, low 21,665.25, close 21,666.75) and the 10:50 bar (high 21,669.00, low 21,664.75, close 21,667.25) both hold, with no 1-minute close below 21,664.00, the far edge. That is the pause, and its high is 21,669.50. The 10:51 bar closes at 21,671.25, above that high. That is the go; the entry is on that close, and the stop rests behind the far edge at 21,662.00: 9.25 points, 37 ticks.

Had the 10:50 bar closed at 21,663.50, below the far edge, there would be no pause and no trigger. The zone gave way on a closed bar, and the order is never armed.

Every part of it is a closed 1-minute bar, so anyone with the chart can mark the pause and the go after the session. The session block, early, mid or late, goes on the trade, so your own record can later show whether the trigger behaves the same across the day. The course writes no separate definition for any block until your record has enough of each to compare.

The rule: two held 1-minute bars inside the zone, then a 1-minute close beyond the pause's extreme in the direction of the read. The entry is on that close and no sooner.

Getting it wrong looks like a buy stop one tick above the pause high, filled by a bar that later closes back inside the zone. What it reveals: you are paying to avoid the cost of the close, which means you trust the poke more than the bar that has to agree with it. The fix: on the plan line, beside the zone, write three boxes: "bar 1 held / bar 2 held / close beyond [price]". The order ticket opens only after the third box is ticked, and the order is sent at market on that close, with the stop attached.

The Resting Limit After Displacement

Some triggers are a fill rather than a close. A resting limit is a buy or sell order waiting inside the zone, and when price comes back and fills it, the fill is the trigger. It counts only when price has left the level first.

Displacement
An impulse away from a level in the direction of the read, before price comes back to the zone. A resting limit is a trigger only after one. This course does not set its size; you write your own minimum on the plan line and record the size of each one.
Resting Limit at Level
A limit order resting inside a zone at a price the branch named, placed only after displacement; the fill is the trigger. A limit placed blind, before any displacement, carries trigger none in the journal. Its price is recorded as the intended price.
Intended Price
The price the plan said you would be filled at, written before the order is sent and recorded in the journal's intended_price field. The actual fill is measured against it on every trade.

Show it on a pd-high zone of 21,690.00 to 21,696.00. Price is accepted above at 10:05. By 10:14 it has run to 21,738.50, more than 40 points above the zone's top, in one leg with no bar back inside. The plan line's minimum was 20 points, so that is displacement. At 10:14 the buy limit goes in at 21,696.00, the near edge for the pullback, with the stop behind the far edge at 21,686.00. The trade note reads "resting-limit, intended 21,696.00, displacement 42.50 points". If price comes back and fills it, that fill is the trigger.

The same limit placed at 21,696.00 the night before, or at 09:58 when price first reached the zone from below, is a blind limit. It may fill, and the trade may even reach its target. Its trigger field still says none, because nothing had happened at the level when the order went in.

One more thing about limits: a simulator fills them on a touch, and a live market often doesn't. A resting-limit trade in sim tells you the price came there. It doesn't tell you that you would have been filled, which is one reason sim trades are counted apart from live ones and never pooled (Module 18).

The rule: a resting limit is a trigger only when it was placed after displacement, at a price the branch named inside the zone, with the stop behind the far edge. Its price is the intended price, recorded on every trade.

Getting it wrong looks like a row of limits parked at every zone before the open, "because that's where it should turn". What it reveals: you want the level to be the trade, so you skip the read and the reply and let a touch decide for you. The fix: no limit ticket is created until the displacement line is written in the trade note with its size in points and its time. A limit that was already working before that line existed is cancelled, or, if it fills, logged with trigger none.

The 9 EMA Pullback

9 EMA Pullback
A continuation-only trigger: in the pullback to the zone, with price at the zone, a bar closes back on the trend side of the 9 EMA; entry on that close. A wick through the average is not the trigger, and an EMA reclaim against the move is not this trigger.
EMA (Exponential Moving Average)
A moving average that weights recent prices more heavily. In this course a moving average is never a level: the 9 EMA pullback is a continuation-only entry trigger (Module 13), judged on a close, and a reclaim against the move is not that trigger.

Write the chart the average is drawn on once, on your plan, and keep it; the examples on this page use the 9 EMA on the 1-minute chart, the same chart Pause N Go is read on. Show it on a pd-vah zone of 21,650.00 to 21,655.00, after acceptance above it. Price pulls back, and from 10:33 the 1-minute bars close below the 9 EMA. At 10:36 a bar trades down to 21,652.75, inside the zone, and closes at 21,658.50, above the average at 21,657.75. That close is the trigger. The stop goes behind the zone's far edge, at 21,648.00, not behind the average: 10.50 points, 42 ticks.

Two things look like this trigger and aren't. A buy limit sitting on the average fills on a wick, before any bar has closed back above it, so it is neither this trigger nor a resting limit at a level. And after a move down, a close back above the 9 EMA is a reclaim against the move, a reversal. It is a level reclaim only if the price it closes back through is a level from TAXONOMY section 4 and the close is a 5-minute one. Otherwise the trigger is none.

The average does one job here: it times an entry in a pullback that already has a zone and a read. It never sets the location, the thesis or the exit.

The rule: the 9 EMA pullback fires only in a continuation pullback, with price at the zone, on a closed bar back on the trend side of the average. The stop is behind the zone's far edge.

Getting it wrong looks like a long on the first close above the 9 EMA after a selloff, in the middle of the range, logged as ema-pullback. What it reveals: the average has become the signal, so you trade it wherever it crosses, and the stop has no structure behind it. The fix: the ema-pullback tag can be written only after two other fields: the location slug of the zone price is in, and the setup trend-continuation with its acceptance close and time. Either one missing, and the trigger is none.

The Level Reclaim

Level Reclaim
A trigger: after price has traded through the zone of a TAXONOMY section 4 level, a 5-minute close back through the whole zone to the side it came from; entry on that close. A close back through anything that is not a section 4 level, such as VWAP or an average, is trigger none.

Show it on a gap-down morning (Module 12). The on-high zone, 21,488.00 to 21,494.00, sits between the open and the PD low, and price has been accepted above it. At 10:02 it trades back down through the whole zone, to 21,481.25. The 10:00 to 10:05 bar closes at 21,490.50, inside the zone; that is not a close below it, so the branch's invalidation hasn't printed. The 10:05 to 10:10 bar closes at 21,497.25, above 21,494.00. That close is the level reclaim, and the entry is on it. The stop rests behind the structure the reclaim answered, below the low of the move through the zone: 21,479.00, 18.25 points, 73 ticks. How far below is Module 14's arithmetic.

Three closes in this course look alike and do different jobs. Keep them apart:

CloseWhat printsWhat it is
Acceptance test (Module 10)A 5-minute close beyond the zone within 15 minutes of the first touchThe read for Trend Continuation. Not an entry.
Reclaim (Module 11)A 1-minute close back through the whole zone after at least two 1-minute closes beyond its far edgePart of the failed breakdown or failed breakout read. Not an entry; the trigger there is a Pause N Go from the reclaimed zone.
Level Reclaim (this page)A 5-minute close back through the whole zone of a section 4 level, after price has traded through itA trigger. The entry is on that close.

The rule: a level reclaim is a 5-minute close back through the whole zone of a section 4 level. It counts only at a level on your list, and only on the close.

Getting it wrong looks like "reclaimed VWAP" or "back above 21,500" in the trigger field, or a buy on the 1-minute bar that pokes back above the zone halfway through the 5-minute bar. What it reveals: any line price crosses back over feels like a level when you want a trade, and the shorter close feels like being early instead of being wrong. The fix: the level-reclaim tag is accepted only when the location field holds a section 4 slug and the trade note gives the 5-minute bar's close time and price ("on-high reclaimed, 5-minute close 21,497.25 at 10:10"). No slug or no close time, and the trigger is none.

Order Flow Confirms; It Never Triggers

The footprint (Module 07) can add evidence to a trigger. It can't stand in for one. A confirmed entry has two parts: a recorded sub-form, and a price event, a bar close or a break of structure.

Aggression Fails At The Level
The order-flow confirmation in this course: the side pushing into a named level is running out. It is recorded with one of four sub-forms (big-orders, failing-delta, delta-divergence, two-bar-exhaustion) on every trade that uses it. It is never a trigger; a price event must also fire.
Confirmation
Order-flow evidence recorded beside a trigger, in the journal's confirmation field: one of the four aggression sub-forms by name, or none. It supports an entry; it never replaces the price event and never changes size.

The four sub-forms, exactly as Module 07 defines them:

Sub-formWhat it is
big-ordersPassive size that holds and prints at the level. Aggressors keep hitting it and price does not get through.
failing-deltaDelta declining on approach. Each bar coming into the level carries less aggression in the direction of the push than the one before.
delta-divergencePrice makes a new extreme on approach to the level and delta does not. Read on the approach, not on arrival.
two-bar-exhaustionA bar with large opposing delta inside it that closes near its extreme in the direction of your read, then a break of that signal bar. For a long: deep selling inside the bar, a close near its high, then a break above it.

Show it on the on-high Pause N Go above. The three 1-minute bars coming down into the zone before the 10:48 touch carry deltas of −142, −88 and −31: failing-delta, read from approach bars. The confirmation field gets failing-delta. The trigger field gets pause-n-go, and the order still waits for the 10:51 close. Had the deltas told you nothing, the confirmation field would say none and the trigger would be the same trigger.

The rule: every trade that uses order flow records the sub-form by name, and also has a trigger that fired at the level. A sub-form with no trigger is logged with trigger none.

Getting it wrong looks like the confirmation field filled in after the trade with whichever sub-form the replay seems to show, or a sell the moment an exhaustion bar closes, before anything breaks. What it reveals: the footprint is either reassurance added afterwards or an entry signal you trust more than price, and either way your record can never show which sub-form meant anything. The fix: the confirmation field is filled before the trigger bar closes, or it is none. Whatever you saw after the close is a note, not a confirmation.

Best Available Fill

Everything above says when. This part is about where you actually got in, and it is the hard one, because it is decided by how you feel when price is leaving without you.

Best Available Fill
The execution leak where the read and the level are right but the fill drifts away from the level, so the stop the structure needs grows wider. It is measured on every trade as the distance between the actual fill and the intended price, in ticks.

The journal records intended_price and actual_fill on every trade, and the gap between them is the measurement: the distance from the intended price to the actual fill, in ticks, counted as positive when the fill is worse for the trade. The grade uses it too. A+ needs all four checks with the fill within 2 ticks of the intended price; every tick past that is the difference between A+ and A, whatever the outcome.

Show it: the stop belongs to the structure, not to your fill. If the zone's far edge says the stop goes at 21,686.00, it goes there whether you were filled at 21,696.00 or at 21,719.00. Filled at 21,696.00, the stop is 40 ticks away. Filled at 21,719.00, it is 132. The same 1R buys less than a third as many contracts, and the target, which hasn't moved, is now a fraction of the R it was. The worked case below runs the whole replay.

The pull to chase is the fear of the move that leaves without you. It is the same fear that makes the first touch look like a trigger, from the other side. If the stop the structure needs suddenly looks impossible, the entry has drifted away from the level.

The rule: write the intended price before every order. Enter only at the zone, on a trigger. Measure the gap on every trade.

Getting it wrong looks like a market buy 20 points above the zone because "it's going without me", then a stop moved up to keep the size, so it sits above the zone with nothing behind it. What it reveals: missing the trade hurts you more than taking a bad one, so the structure gets traded away to stay in the move. It breaches the stop check of the grade, and a breach of that check is a C. The fix: no market order is sent while price is outside the zone; a close-based trigger fires only with price at the zone, and a limit rests at the intended price. Once a week, list every fill gap over 2 ticks, with the time and the trigger beside it.

The level asks; the trigger is the market's reply, a closed bar or a fill you can point to, and order flow can only add a note to it. No reply, no entry. Where you get filled is part of the reply: the stop belongs to the structure, so a fill away from the level changes everything except the stop.

Which Trigger Did You Actually Have?

Take your last ten trades. Score each one a point for each line that is true:

  1. Event. The trigger field holds one of the four slugs, and you can point to the bar that closed or the fill that filled.
  2. Place. Price was at a named zone from TAXONOMY section 4 when the trigger fired.
  3. Order. A close-based trigger was entered on the closed bar; a resting limit went in after the displacement line was written.
  4. Confirmation. The confirmation field shows a sub-form by name or none, filled before the trigger bar closed.
  5. Fill. The intended price was written before the order, and the fill landed within 2 ticks of it.
  6. Re-entry. If it was a second entry at the same setup, it had its own fresh trigger, and the invalidation had not printed.

Lines 1 and 2 override the score. A trade that misses line 1 had no trigger; a trade that misses line 2 was a trigger without a location. The threshold: if two or more of your ten miss line 1 or line 2, nothing else on this list matters yet: for the next ten trades, the trigger line goes in the trade note before the ticket is armed. If three or more of your ten miss line 5, Best Available Fill is your leak, and the fill-gap list goes on your weekly review (Module 18). Otherwise, find the line the most trades missed and write it on your pre-market sheet as a check.

Before Your Next Session

Apply This Now

Next session, before each order, write one line: the trigger slug and the intended price. "pause-n-go, intended 24,102.75." After the fill, write the gap in ticks beside it. That's the whole assignment.

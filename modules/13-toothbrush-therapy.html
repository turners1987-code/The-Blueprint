The Basics You Skip On A Busy Morning

You know the morning. You have read every page on levels, reads and triggers, and on Tuesday you still sat down at 09:27 with no idea written, took a trade from a price you noticed, nudged the stop when it came close, and never graded it. None of that was a gap in knowledge. Each one was a basic you knew and skipped, on the morning it mattered.

Brushing your teeth gets done because you don't decide to do it. It happens at the same point every day, and missing it feels wrong. This page sets out eight trading habits to run the same way: not strategies, not secrets, just routines done every session. Each one is written as a line on your sheet or in your journal, so you can see whether you ran it, rather than trust that you did.

The eight fall into four parts of the day, mechanical first: before the open, at the entry, after the exit, and last, the people around your desk, which is about you rather than the chart.

Before The Open: Your Idea, Two Plans And One Goal

1. Know Your Idea

Idea
A conditional statement written before the session: a thesis in if/then form at a named level, the invalidation that ends it, and a conviction grade of strong, moderate or thin. It describes what you will do if price reaches a place, not where price will go.

Before the open, write the thesis, the invalidation and the conviction at a named level. No day-type call: "trend day" and "range day" are labels for a whole session that no price at 09:45 can prove wrong. The conviction is counted, not felt: one point each for the three checks in Module 09 (zone, path, room and clock), so three is strong, two moderate, one or none thin.

The habit: every session, the idea is on paper before 09:25, at a slug, with an invalidation price and a counted conviction.

Getting it wrong looks like "feels like a trend day up" written where the idea should be. What it reveals: a forecast came first, and every bar will be read to agree with it. The fix: write the idea backwards, invalidation first, and strike any line on the sheet that has no slug and no price in it.

2. Have A Plan

Plan
What you write before a session: one or more branches and a nothing case. It says what you will do at each place price can go. It never says where price will go.

The script is "If price does X at [slug] and [trigger] fires, you do Y; you are wrong on [price, on a candle close]." Write it down. Say it out loud in the rehearsal. You walk in with a branch, not a reaction. The full shape of a branch, and the test each line has to pass, is Module 15's.

The habit: every session, at least one branch is written in that form before 09:25.

Getting it wrong looks like "looking for longs" or "buy the dip". What it reveals: the plan names a direction and no place, so the session decides where you trade. The fix: every branch begins with "If", names a slug before any other word, and carries an invalidation price; a line missing any of the three is crossed out before 09:25.

3. Have A Second Plan

Nothing Case
The line of the plan that names where no branch applies, written as a price range with two numbers, plus anything the plan has no branch for. In the nothing case the action is no trade.

Price can reach the other side; write a branch there too. The second plan is not a fallback story about what to do when the first one fails. It is one branch on each side of price, each with its own invalidation, plus a nothing case for everything between and beyond them (Module 15). The script for the other side is the same: "If price does X at [the slug on the other side] and [trigger] fires, you do Y; you are wrong on [price]."

The habit: every session, a branch on each side of price and a nothing case with two numbers ending "no trade".

Getting it wrong looks like one branch, long, and nothing written below price. What it reveals: the plan covers what you hope price will do, so the side you didn't write gets filled by impulse. The fix: the sheet has two idea lines and a nothing-case line printed on it; if you choose not to trade one side, that side's price range goes into the nothing case by name.

4. Set Your Process Goal

Pick one weakness, from your last weekly review (Module 18), not from yesterday's worst trade. Set one goal for it. Say it out loud before you trade: "Today you enter only after the trigger fires at a level on the plan." A process goal is about what you do, never about what you make.

The habit: one process goal, one sentence, at the top of the sheet, said aloud before 09:25, and marked kept or not kept in the end-of-day routine.

Getting it wrong looks like three goals at once, or "make 2R today". What it reveals: the goal is a wish about the result, so nothing about your behaviour can be checked at the close. The fix: the goal line has room for one sentence, and it must name an action the journal records, such as the trigger field, the exit tag or the stop price. Keep the same goal until the weekly review shows it kept for a full week.

At The Entry: Your Setups And Your Stop

5. Only Trade Your Setups

No Setup
The journal tag for a trade with no named location and read behind it: a trade taken on feel. An idea without a written invalidation is No Setup, however good it sounds.

Not someone else's. Not a gut feeling. Yours. Written down. Recorded in your journal. A setup is yours when it sits on your plan as a branch and in your journal as a slug, with its own count of trades.

The habit: every entry's setup and location match a branch written before 09:25.

Getting it wrong looks like a midday trade from a price you found on the screen, or one copied from a chat. What it reveals: the screen became the plan the moment the plan went quiet. The fix: before the order is armed, read the branch it comes from aloud. No branch to read, no order; a trade taken anyway is tagged No Setup, and the weekly review counts it.

6. Honour Your Stop

Broke Rule
The journal tag for breaking a hard rule: an entry outside the trading window or from 15:55 ET, a position held through a Tier One release or still open at T−5, a stop widened after entry, a new entry with no post-loss reset, or trading past the kill switch.

Always. No exceptions. Ever. The stop is a resting order behind the zone's far edge, sent with the entry (Module 14). It is never widened, and it is never pulled to breakeven by hand or by automation; the one planned scale is the only change to the position the plan allows.

The habit: every trade has its stop resting from the moment of entry, at the price the plan wrote, until the trade ends.

Getting it wrong looks like a stop dragged two points further away as price closes in, or tightened to breakeven "to be safe". What it reveals: the stop is being treated as an opinion you can revise, so the risk you sized for is not the risk you carry. The fix: the order template sends the stop with the entry as a bracket, with automatic breakeven off. A widened stop is tagged Broke Rule; a stop moved closer and hit is an exit outside the Pass list, tagged Too Early.

After The Exit: Review And Grade Every Trade

7. Review And Grade Every Trade

Grade (A+ / A / B / C)
The entry-plan score given to every trade at entry, before it resolves, from four checks: the location is a named level, the trigger fired before entry, the stop is behind structure, and the size matches the plan for that stop. A+ is all four with a fill within 2 ticks of the intended price; A is all four; B is three of four with no breach of the stop or size check; C is two or fewer, or any breach of those two. C is no trade.

A+, A, B or C, graded on the plan at entry, not on the outcome. Then, after the exit, the execution mark: Pass or Fail, did the exit follow the plan. One line minimum. Every trade. Every day. Module 18 lays out every field.

The habit: every trade is graded at entry and marked in the end-of-day routine, the same day, with one line in your own words.

Getting it wrong looks like grading only the losers, or a week of trades marked on Sunday from memory. What it reveals: the review is chasing pain rather than recording decisions, so the trades that taught the wrong lesson, the C that won, never get looked at. The fix: the grade field is filled before the exit, and the end-of-day routine (Module 17) isn't finished while any trade of the day has an empty mark.

Around The Desk: Find Your People

8. Find Your People

Find a trading partner or group who speaks the language, after the session; the sheet is yours. Before 09:25 nothing from anyone else goes on it (Module 09). What you share is your record, not your calls: once a week, the review's counts, your grades, your marks, your Too Early count and your Broke Rule ledger, and you read theirs. The community keeps you honest because it sees the same fields every week, including the weeks you would rather not show.

This is the hard one, because the pull toward other traders is strongest on the mornings your own sheet looks uncertain, and a confident call feels like help.

The habit: outside views stay closed until after the close; once a week, you exchange review fields with a partner or group.

Getting it wrong looks like opening the chat at 08:50 and adding a branch at 09:24 that matches a post. What it reveals: you are looking for someone else to carry the uncertainty, so the idea on the sheet is no longer yours to grade. The fix: chats, feeds and notes stay closed until the mental close; the weekly exchange uses the review sheet's lines and nothing else, no screenshots of P&L and no calls for tomorrow.

A habit counts only when it leaves a line on paper every session, whatever the day did. When these eight run without thinking, they stop costing you attention, and the record shows whether you ran them.

Worked Case: One Session, Eight Lines

Illustration · not a trade record

A composite MNQ session, built to show each habit as a line on paper. It belongs to no one, the prices are invented for the arithmetic, and points are index points on the MNQ chart; one tick is 0.25 points. The morning is the filled sheet from Module 16; last week's review is the one in Module 18.

1. Know your idea (on the sheet by 09:21). Idea 1: if price accepts above the pd-high zone (21,454 to 21,460) and a Pause N Go fires on the pullback, then prior-week-high, 21,568; wrong on a 5-minute close back below 21,454; moderate: one level in the zone. No day-type word anywhere on the sheet.

2 and 3. Two plans. Idea 2: if price accepts below the pd-val zone (21,352 to 21,358) and a Pause N Go fires on the pullback, then pd-low; wrong on a 5-minute close back above 21,358; thin: one level in the zone, and the htf-lvn sits in the path. Nothing case: between 21,358 and 21,454 without acceptance outside it, no trade.

4. Process goal, from last week's review, said aloud at 09:15: "Today you exit only at the target, the planned scale, the stop, or for one of the four reasons."

5. Only your setups. First touch of the pd-high zone at 09:58, test 1. The 5-minute bar closing at 10:05 closes at 21,466, above 21,460, seven minutes after the first touch: acceptance (Module 10). At 10:09 price pulls back to 21,459, the first touch of test 2; 10:10 and 10:11 hold inside the zone, highs no higher than 21,460, lows no lower than 21,455. At 10:12 a bar closes at 21,462, above the pause's high: Pause N Go, entry long at 21,462, filled at the intended price. Setup trend-continuation, location pd-high: it matches Idea 1. At 12:40 a fast move off an unmarked price gets one line in the notes and no order.

6. Honour your stop. The bracket goes with the entry: stop 21,452, two points behind the far edge, now the bottom of the zone, 21,454; 10 points, 40 ticks. Target 21,568, 106 points away, 10.6R; planned scale at 2R, 21,482. At 10:31 the scale fills: half off at +2R. The runner's stop stays at 21,452, untouched. At 11:20 price trades back through the zone and the stop fills on the runner: −1R on that half. The trade: 0.5 × 2R + 0.5 × (−1R) = +0.5R.

7. Review and grade. Grade at entry, 10:12: named level, trigger fired, stop behind structure, size from the table, fill gap 0 ticks: A+. Mark at 16:18: Pass, the planned scale and the stop. Tags: test-2, block-early. Process goal: kept.

8. Find your people. No chat opened before the mental close at 16:40. On Sunday the week's review lines go to a partner: grades, marks, the Too Early count, the Broke Rule ledger.

Eight lines, all on paper. Where price went after 11:20 is not on any of them.

Hypothetical illustration. Hypothetical results have inherent limits: they are not actual trading and do not reflect real fills, slippage or the pressure of risk, and no representation is made that any account will achieve similar results.

Notice what the habits didn't need. None of them asked whether the day would be good, and the runner's stop-out cost the same attention as the scale: none. The day was scored on eight lines you could check, and the result sits in the journal beside them, not above them.

Which Habit Is Yours?

Take your last five sessions. Score one point for each habit that left its line on paper in all five:

  1. Know your idea: a thesis at a slug, an invalidation price and a counted conviction, before 09:25.
  2. Have a plan: a branch in the "If price does X at [slug] and [trigger] fires" form.
  3. Have a second plan: a branch on the other side of price and a nothing case with two numbers.
  4. Process goal: one sentence at the top of the sheet, marked kept or not kept at the close.
  5. Only your setups: every entry matched a branch; no No Setup tags.
  6. Honour your stop: every stop rested from entry to exit at its planned price; no Broke Rule for a widened stop.
  7. Review and grade: every trade graded at entry and marked the same day.
  8. Find your people: no outside view opened before the close; review fields exchanged once that week.

8 of 8: the habits are running; keep them running. 6 or 7: the missing habit is next week's process goal. Under 6: start with lines 1 and 7, because one sets what you will do and the other records whether you did it. The override: a miss on line 6 makes the stop next week's process goal, whatever the total.

Before Your Next Session

Apply This Now

Tomorrow, before 09:25, write one process goal at the top of your sheet, one sentence that starts "Today you", and say it aloud once. That's the whole assignment.

MODULE 10 The Blueprint

Trend Continuation

Trend continuation is not a guess that a move will keep going; it is a test the level sets, and price either passes it on a closed bar inside fifteen minutes or there is no setup to trade.

Copies this lesson as Markdown to paste into any AI assistant

The Break You Bought At The Top

You know this one. Price runs at yesterday's high, pokes through it, and the bar is green and getting longer. You buy it, because the break is happening and you don't want to watch it leave. Two minutes later price is back inside yesterday's range, and your stop, set a few points under your fill because there was nothing else to put it behind, is gone. Twenty minutes after that, price goes through the high again and keeps going, without you.

Both halves of that morning have the same cause. You traded the movement through the level instead of the level's answer to it. This page teaches the answer: a closed bar beyond the zone, inside a fixed time, and then a trigger on the pullback, with the stop behind the zone and the target at the next level, all written before the order goes in.

There are six parts, mechanical first: the acceptance test, the first touch and the test number, the trigger in the pullback, the stop, the target with its one planned scale, and the move that leaves without you. The last one is about you rather than the chart, which is why it comes last.

The Acceptance Test

Every trade in this course traces one chain: idea → setup (location + read) → trigger → construction (level → stop → risk → size → entry). This page is the setup tier for one of its two outcomes. Price at a level either holds or fails. When the move through the level holds, the setup is Trend Continuation. When it fails, the setup is Range Rejection (Module 11). Same level, same analysis, opposite answers.

Setup
The second tier: a named location plus the read of what price is doing there, judged on a closed bar. Price at a level either holds or fails; the read says which. It is recorded in two journal fields, the setup (the read) and the location (the slug).
Acceptance
Price building time and volume at a new price instead of passing through it. Acceptance moves value; a quick visit does not. The mechanical acceptance test this course uses at a level is taught in Module 10.

That test is the whole of this setup's read, and it is the same at every level on your list in TAXONOMY section 4: a prior-day extreme, a prior-day value-area high or low, settlement, an overnight extreme, a prior-week extreme, a higher-timeframe node, or an in-session level once it has formed.

Acceptance Test
The read for Trend Continuation: a 5-minute close beyond the zone within 15 minutes of the first touch. A wick through the zone is not acceptance, and neither is a close beyond it that comes after the 15 minutes.
Trend Continuation
The setup where the level holds: price tests a level and is accepted beyond it, a 5-minute close beyond the zone within 15 minutes of the first touch. The trade goes in the direction of acceptance, with the stop behind the zone's far edge on the far side of acceptance. Journal slug: trend-continuation.

Show it on a pd-high zone of 24,312.00 to 24,318.00, with price arriving from below. The first touch prints at 09:52. Fifteen minutes from 09:52 is 10:07, so the 5-minute closes that count are 09:55, 10:00 and 10:05. If any of them closes above 24,318.00, the top of the zone, price has been accepted above. A 1-minute spike to 24,326 that closes back at 24,316 is not acceptance. Neither is a 10:15 close above the zone; by then the window has shut.

"Beyond the zone" means beyond the whole band, not beyond the price you think of as the level. A close inside the zone is a close inside the zone, whichever edge it sits near.

The rule: the read is acceptance only when a 5-minute bar closes beyond the zone within 15 minutes of the first touch. Anything else, at that test, is no Trend Continuation.

Getting it wrong looks like "it broke the high" said about a bar that is still open, or about a wick. What it reveals: you are reading the bar you want instead of the bar that closed, so the read changes every few seconds with your hopes. The fix: the setup field in the journal is filled only with a time and a price: "trend-continuation, 5-minute close 24,331.25 at 10:00". If you can't write the close and its time, the field stays empty and no trigger is looked for.

The First Touch And The Test Number

First Touch
The first print inside a zone on a test of it. Its time starts the 15-minute acceptance clock and is written down when it prints.

The clock needs a start, and the start is a price you can point at: the first trade at the zone's near edge. Module 17 puts an alert on every zone's near edge, so the first touch is the moment that alert sounds. Write the time, and the deadline next to it: "pd-high, touch 09:52, deadline 10:07". From that line on, the read is a yes or a no that the chart will hand you by a known minute.

Test Number
How many times price has come to a zone in the session: the first touch of the zone that session is test 1, the next is test 2, and so on. It is recorded on every trade at a level.

Price can come back to a zone more than once in a day. Whether a second test of a level behaves like the first is a question only your own record can answer, and it can answer it only if every trade carries the number. The test number is how the first-touch idea, the belief many traders hold that a level's first test is the one that matters, becomes something you can count instead of something you repeat. Write it whether or not you think it matters.

The rule: at the first touch, write the time, the deadline 15 minutes later, and the test number. Every trade at a level records its test number.

Getting it wrong looks like a journal where the test number is filled in on the evening review, from memory, or left blank on the trades that lost. What it reveals: the number is being treated as decoration instead of data, so the question it exists to answer can never be answered from your record. The fix: the test number goes in the trade note before the order, next to the touch time. A trade note without one is incomplete, and the trade is reviewed as if the setup field were empty.

The Trigger Lives In The Pullback

Acceptance tells you which way the level answered. It doesn't tell you when to enter. On this page the acceptance close is the read, not the entry. The entry comes after it, when price pulls back toward the zone it was accepted beyond and something happens there.

Trigger
The price event at the level that tells you to enter now, such as Pause N Go, a resting limit after displacement, or a level reclaim on a 5-minute close (Module 13). Order flow can confirm a trigger; it is never the trigger.
Pause N Go
Pause: after the first touch of a level, at least two consecutive 1-minute bars hold inside the zone with no 1-minute close through its far edge. Resumption: a 1-minute close beyond the pause's extreme in the direction of the read; entry on that close; stop behind the far edge. The session block (early / mid / late) is recorded on every trade.

Show it on the same pd-high zone. After the 10:00 acceptance close, price runs to 24,352.50 and comes back. At 10:14 it touches 24,318.00, the top of the zone, which is now the near edge because price is arriving from above. The 10:15 and 10:16 bars both stay in the zone with no 1-minute close below 24,312.00, the far edge. That is the pause; its high is 24,320.25. The 10:17 bar closes at 24,322.50, above that high. That is the go, and the entry is on that close.

The session block, early, mid or late, is recorded on every Pause N Go trade, and on every other trade, so your record can later show whether the trigger behaves the same across the day.

EMA (Exponential Moving Average)
A moving average that weights recent prices more heavily. In this course a moving average is never a level: the 9 EMA pullback is a continuation-only entry trigger (Module 13), judged on a close, and a reclaim against the move is not that trigger.

The 9 EMA pullback is the other trigger this setup can use. In the pullback after acceptance, with price at the zone, a bar closes back on the trend side of the 9 EMA; the close is the trigger, never the wick through the average. The zone is still the location and the stop still goes behind its far edge. The average only times the entry. Module 13 teaches both triggers in full, with the rest of the trigger list.

Order flow from Module 07, such as failing delta on the pullback, can confirm either trigger, and its sub-form is recorded. It never replaces the close.

The rule: after acceptance, the entry is a named trigger that fires in the pullback, with price at the zone. No pullback to the zone, no trade.

Getting it wrong looks like a long at the high of the acceptance bar, or three bars later, 30 points above the zone, because the move "confirmed". What it reveals: you treated the read as permission to enter anywhere in the direction it pointed, so the trade has the level's direction and none of its structure. Your stop sits in the middle of nowhere and its distance is a guess. The fix: the order ticket is armed only after two things are written in the trade note: the acceptance close with its time, and the trigger you are waiting for with its price ("1-minute close above 24,320.25"). No second line, no armed ticket.

The Stop Goes Behind The Far Edge

Far Edge
The edge of a zone on the far side from where price arrives. For a level above current price it is the top of the zone; for a level below, the bottom. The stop goes behind it.

In a continuation trade price arrives at the zone twice, from opposite sides. On the first touch it comes from below (for a long) and the far edge is the top. That is the edge acceptance has to close beyond. On the pullback it comes back from above, and now the far edge is the bottom: the side of the zone on the far side of acceptance. That is the edge your stop goes behind.

Show it: the pd-high zone is 24,312.00 to 24,318.00, the entry is 24,322.50, and the stop is 24,310.00, two points behind 24,312.00 and below both pause lows. The risk is 12.50 points, 50 ticks. If price trades through the whole zone and fills that stop, the accepted level has stopped holding, and the trade is over by the same fact that started it.

Invalidation
The named price, judged on a candle close of a stated timeframe, at which a branch's thesis is wrong. It sits at or behind the zone's far edge. Written before the session, never moved during it.

The branch also carries its invalidation: "a 5-minute close back below 24,312.00". The stop is the resting order; the invalidation is the close that ends the thesis whether or not the stop has filled. A thesis broken on a candle close is one of the four reasons to flatten by hand in Module 02.

The stop is a resting order, sent with the entry, every time. If your own stop floor for the time of day is wider than the far edge, the stop goes out to the floor and the size falls; a wider stop changes the size, never the risk. The arithmetic, the 2:1 check and the size from the grade are Module 14's.

The rule: the stop is a resting order behind the zone's far edge on the far side of acceptance, sent with the entry, and never widened.

Getting it wrong looks like a stop under the low of the trigger bar, inside the zone, because it made the size bigger. What it reveals: the stop was chosen for the size you wanted, not for the structure you are trading, so ordinary noise inside the zone can end a trade whose level never broke. The fix: write the far-edge price and the stop price on the plan line before the session, as a pair: "far edge 24,312.00, stop 24,310.00". The stop on the ticket must match the line, or the order isn't sent.

The Target Is The Next Level

Target
A level a trade aims at, written before entry. It says where price may travel to; it is never a reason to enter and never a direction.

The target is a level from your list in the direction of the trade, normally the next one, written on the branch before the session and untouched once the trade is on. It is not a number of R. A fixed 2R or 3R target is a price nobody else is looking at. A level is a place where something already happened and may happen again. Where the branch aims past a nearer level, that nearer level is in the path, and the path check in the conviction count (Module 09) marks it.

Show it: from the pd-high entry at 24,322.50, the next level up is the prior-week-high zone, 24,388.00 to 24,396.00, and the target is its near edge, 24,388.00. That is 65.50 points of room against 12.50 points of risk, about 5.2 to 1. The 2:1 check is a filter, not a target: it says whether the trade is allowed, not where it ends.

Planned Scale
The one exit, besides the target and the stop, planned before entry: half the position off at 2R, the rest held as the runner to the target level. Any other breakeven move or trail is Too Early.
Runner
The half of the position left on after the planned scale. It is held to the target level with the original resting stop, and its only other exits are the four reasons to flatten by hand in Module 02.

On the pd-high trade, 2R is 25.00 points, so the scale is at 24,347.50 and the runner aims at 24,388.00. The stop stays at 24,310.00 for the runner. Platform automation is off and the breakeven key is unbound, because moving the stop to entry after the scale is exactly the kind of exit the plan didn't contain. Adding to the trade on the way is not part of it either: an add is a new trade, with its own level, trigger and stop.

Too Early
The journal tag for a manual exit taken for any reason other than the four. Its execution mark is Fail, whatever the trade went on to do.

The rule: the target is a level written before entry; the exits are the target, the planned scale, the stop, and the four reasons in Module 02. Nothing else.

Getting it wrong looks like taking everything off at 1.5R "because it stalled", or dragging the stop up behind each new swing. What it reveals: the exit is being run by your fear of giving back open profit, which means the plan stopped being the plan the moment the trade went your way. The fix: the target order and the scale order are sent with the entry, as resting orders, so the only thing your hand can do afterwards is cancel one. Any cancel, and any stop moved for a reason not written at entry, is tagged Too Early.

The Move That Leaves Without You

Sometimes price is accepted beyond the level and never comes back. It closes above the zone at 10:00, keeps going, and by 10:30 it is at the target without a single pullback into the zone. There was a read. There was never a trigger. That is no trade.

This is the hard part, and it is not on the chart. Watching a branch you wrote come true without you feels like a loss, and the urge it creates is to make sure it never happens again, by entering earlier next time: on the acceptance bar, on the first touch, on the poke through. Each of those trades the movement instead of the level, which is the morning at the top of this page.

A trade you didn't take cost you nothing. The record of it, though, is worth something: a branch that accepted and left without a pullback is a count your journal can keep, and over enough sessions it tells you how often your setup offers no trigger at all.

The rule: acceptance without a pullback to the zone is no trade. It is marked on the chart and noted in the mark-up, never chased.

Getting it wrong looks like a long 40 points above the zone at 10:20, entered because "it's not coming back", with the stop wherever the size allows. What it reveals: missing a move hurts you more than taking a bad trade, so the next missed move will buy you a trade with nothing under its stop. It is tagged mid-range, and it is No Setup. The fix: the order ticket stays unarmed while price is outside the zone. When a branch accepts and leaves, write one line in the trade note instead of an order: "pd-high accepted 10:00, no pullback, no trade". That line is the trade you took.

The level asks the question and a 5-minute close inside 15 minutes answers it; the trade comes only on the pullback after that answer, with the stop behind the zone and the target at a level you wrote down first. Without the close there is no setup, and without the pullback there is no trade.

Worked Cases: One Test, Three Levels

Three composite MNQ sessions with identical mechanics: the same acceptance test, the same Pause N Go on the pullback, the same stop behind the far edge, the same target rule and planned scale. Only the level and the context change. They belong to no one, the prices are invented for the arithmetic, and points are index points on the MNQ chart; one tick is 0.25 points.

Illustration · not a trade record

Case 1: pd-high, long. Context at 09:05: price 24,281.00, inside the PD range and below the pd-high zone; no Tier One release in the window.

The branch, written at 09:15. "Invalidation: a 5-minute close back below 24,312.00. If price accepts above the pd-high zone (24,312.00 to 24,318.00) and a Pause N Go fires on the pullback into the zone, then prior-week-high, 24,388.00. Conviction: moderate: one level in the zone." The checks: zone, one level only, no; path, no level between 24,318.00 and 24,388.00, yes; room and clock, 24,388.00 sits inside the room frame and no Tier One falls in the window, yes. Two of three.

Level and read. First touch 09:52, test 1, deadline 10:07. The 09:55 to 10:00 bar closes at 24,331.25, above 24,318.00, eight minutes after the touch. Accepted above. Setup: trend-continuation; location: pd-high.

Trigger. Price runs to 24,352.50 and pulls back; it touches 24,318.00 at 10:14. Pause: 10:15 (high 24,319.50, low 24,313.25, close 24,315.00) and 10:16 (high 24,320.25, low 24,314.00, close 24,317.75), no 1-minute close below 24,312.00. The 10:17 bar closes at 24,322.50, above the pause's high of 24,320.25. Trigger: pause-n-go.

Construction. Stop 24,310.00, behind the far edge of 24,312.00. Risk 12.50 points, 50 ticks. Target 24,388.00, 65.50 points away, about 5.2 to 1, which clears 2:1. Planned scale at 2R, 24,347.50. Size from the grade, using the table in Module 14. Entry on the 10:17 close, filled at the intended price. Grade at entry: all four checks, fill within 2 ticks: A+.

Outcome. 10:29: the scale fills at 24,347.50, half the position at +2R. The stop stays at 24,310.00. 10:52: the runner's target fills at 24,388.00, +5.24R on that half. The position closes at about +3.6R before costs. Execution mark: Pass.

After the exit. Price traded on to 24,409.75 by 11:20 and was back at 24,371.00 by noon. Neither move belonged to the trade. After the exit is not your money.

Size from the grade, using the table in Module 14; strong conviction does not change it. Entry on the 10:44 close, filled one tick away at 23,842.75. Grade at entry: A+.

From the 23,842.75 fill that is 53 ticks against the 52 the size was built on: −1.02R before costs. The 11:00 bar closes at 23,861.50, above 23,854.00, so the thesis was broken on its own close as well. The trade was built to plan and exited to plan. It gets no negative tag. Execution mark: Pass.

After the exit. Price rotated back up to 23,912.00 by 11:40. The level that had been accepted below was traded back through and given up; what that failure means is the read Module 11 teaches. The branch ended on the 11:00 close, so there was no second trade: a re-entry needs the same setup and a fresh trigger, and the setup was gone.

Case 3: developing-vpoc, short that never came. Context: the plan's in-session line, written before 09:30, names developing-vpoc at the trigger price. The branch, written at 12:50. "Invalidation: a 5-minute close back above 24,213.00. If price accepts below the developing-vpoc zone (24,207.00 to 24,213.00) and a Pause N Go fires on the pullback into the zone, then pd-vah, 24,186.00. Conviction: thin: one level in the zone, settlement in the path." The checks: zone, one level, no; path, settlement (24,195.00 to 24,199.00) sits between, no; room and clock, yes. One of three. The stop, had a trigger fired, would sit behind 24,213.00, the zone's far edge on the far side of acceptance.

Level and read. First touch 12:52 at the top of the zone, test 1, deadline 13:07. The 12:50 to 12:55 bar closes at 24,209.25, inside. In the next bar a 1-minute probe reaches 24,203.50, below the zone, and the 12:55 to 13:00 bar closes at 24,208.75, inside. The 13:00 to 13:05 bar closes at 24,211.50, inside. The next 5-minute close is at 13:10, after the deadline. The developing VPOC stayed at 24,210.00 throughout. No acceptance.

Trigger, stop, target. None used. With no read, there is no setup, so no trigger is looked for, no stop is placed and the target is never in play. The note in the journal: "developing-vpoc, test 1, touch 12:52, not accepted by 13:07, no trade".

After the watch. Price rose from the zone to 24,246.50 by 13:40. The probe to 24,203.50 is what a tap through a zone looks like on a 1-minute chart: real, fast, and not a close. A level that holds against the push into it is the context read of Module 11, which carries no entry on this page.

Notice what stayed the same. Each branch was written before price got to the zone, with its invalidation first. Each read was a 5-minute close checked against a deadline written at the touch. Each trigger was the same two held bars and one close, and each stop sat behind the far edge on the far side of acceptance. The strong branch lost, the moderate one reached its target, and the thin one never happened. Conviction didn't change the size in any of them, and the result didn't change the grade.

Which Part Do You Skip?

Take your last ten trades tagged trend-continuation, or your last ten breaks of a level you traded if you have never used the tag. Score each one a point for each line that is true:

  1. Read. A 5-minute bar closed beyond the whole zone within 15 minutes of the first touch, and you can name the bar.
  2. Touch. The first-touch time and the test number were written before the order.
  3. Trigger. The entry came on a named trigger that fired in the pullback, with price at the zone, not on the acceptance bar or away from the zone.
  4. Stop. A resting stop sat behind the zone's far edge on the far side of acceptance, sent with the entry, never widened.
  5. Target. The target was a level written before entry, and the only exits were the target, the planned scale, the stop and the four reasons in Module 02.

Lines 1 and 3 override the score. A trade that misses line 1 had no setup; a trade that misses line 3 had no trigger at the level. The threshold: if two or more of your ten miss line 1 or line 3, fix those before your next continuation trade: no setup field without a close and its time, and no armed ticket while price is outside the zone. If none do, find the line from 2, 4 and 5 that the most trades missed; if three or more missed it, it goes on your pre-market sheet as a written check.

Before Your Next Session

Apply This Now

Next session, the first time a near-edge alert sounds at one of your branch zones, write one line before you do anything else: the slug, the test number, the touch time and the deadline. "pd-high, test 1, touch 09:52, deadline 10:07." That's the whole assignment.
